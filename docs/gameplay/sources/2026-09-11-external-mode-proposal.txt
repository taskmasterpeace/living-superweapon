You have enough mechanics already. The missing piece is **a ruleset that makes every power level matter in the same battle**.

Right now you have the ingredients for something much more interesting than “superheroes fighting”: **combined-arms warfare where living superweapons change the strategic rules of the battlefield.**

I’d architect **Power World** around that.

## 1. The core fantasy

Don't balance Batman-level soldiers and Goku-level superweapons by pretending their damage numbers are comparable.

That's the wrong problem.

Make them occupy different layers of warfare.

| Layer               | Battlefield role                                    |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Infantry            | capture, infiltrate, interrogate, sabotage, recover |
| Enhanced soldiers   | elite infantry, anti-super tactics                  |
| Vehicles            | mobility, fire support, logistics                   |
| Tanks               | area control / anti-ground                          |
| Helicopters         | transport / pursuit / anti-infantry                 |
| Jets                | interception / bombing / anti-super                 |
| Emplacements        | powerful defensive weapons                          |
| Living Superweapons | battlefield-changing strategic assets               |

A Superman-level character might annihilate 30 soldiers.

But Superman **cannot simultaneously protect the cloning facility, stop a spy, intercept a bomber, retrieve biological material and defend three objectives**.

That's where your game appears.

---

# 2. The lore system that ties everything together

I'd make the cloning technology the center of the entire universe.

### CLONE RESERVE

Each faction owns a **Cloning Complex**.

Instead of infinite respawning:

**Clone Reserve: 250**

Every ordinary soldier death:

**−1 Clone**

Enhanced soldier:

**−3**

Specialist:

**−5**

Living Superweapon:

Doesn't consume normal clone stock. They're unique strategic units with their own regeneration/redeployment rules.

Now death actually matters without forcing players to sit out.

And destroying infrastructure can affect:

* respawn speed
* available classes
* clone reserves
* enhancements
* vehicle crews

Suddenly bombing a cloning laboratory has a military purpose.

# 3. Your blood idea should become a major system

This may be one of the strongest ideas in the game.

Don't call it simply blood.

Call the gameplay resource something like:

**GENETIC MATERIAL / BIO-MASS / WEAPON GENOME / POWER STRAIN**

A living superweapon gets killed.

The body becomes a temporary **strategic objective**.

### Example

**SUPERWEAPON KILLED**

> BIOLOGICAL ASSET DOWN
> Genome integrity: 94%
> Enemy recovery team inbound.

Now BOTH teams have different objectives.

Enemy:

**EXTRACT THE GENOME**

Friendly:

**DENY RECOVERY**

And denial shouldn't just mean shooting the body.

You have options.

* Recover the body yourself.
* Incinerate it.
* Destroy it with artillery.
* Airstrike the corpse.
* Extract the genome first.
* Carry the body away.
* Contaminate the sample.
* Defend it until degradation reaches 0%.

That creates insane emergent moments.

Imagine:

A superweapon gets killed 2 km from base.

Enemy helicopters arrive.

Your soldiers establish a perimeter.

A player jumps into an AA cannon.

Enemy superweapon comes flying over the mountain.

Friendly jet screams overhead.

Some poor infantryman is physically dragging the corpse toward an armored vehicle.

**That's your game.**

---

# 4. Enhanced soldiers

Extracted genome goes back to:

### GENETICS LAB

The faction researches that superweapon.

Suppose the dead character had:

**Optical Energy Projection**

Research eventually unlocks:

**Optical Strain I**

Your clone soldiers can now spawn with weak eye beams.

Not superhero strength.

More like:

Normal rifle soldier:

`Rifle + Grenade`

Enhanced clone:

`Rifle + weak optical beam`

Further research:

`Optical Strain II`

Eventually specialized units appear.

### Power inheritance should be imperfect.

That matters enormously.

Superweapon:

████████████████████ **100%**

Enhanced clone:

███ **15%**

So killing Superman doesn't give your entire army Superman.

It gives your scientists enough biological information to manufacture **inferior derivatives**.

That creates technological escalation during matches/campaigns.

---

# 5. The morality idea: don't make it morality

Your GTA idea is good, but I'd change the framing.

Don't call it Good/Evil.

Make it:

# ESCALATION

The battlefield reacts to what you're doing.

Killing enemy soldiers?

Normal warfare.

Massacring civilians?

**Escalation increases.**

Destroying civilian neighborhoods?

Higher.

Executing prisoners?

Higher.

Using superweapons indiscriminately?

Much higher.

Why should the player care?

Because **escalation gives the opposing faction emergency authorization/resources.**

Example:

### ESCALATION 0

Normal operations.

### ESCALATION I — ALERT

Enemy receives additional intelligence.

### ESCALATION II — MOBILIZATION

Enemy clone production +20%.

### ESCALATION III — EMERGENCY POWERS

Heavy weapons authorized.

### ESCALATION IV — TOTAL WAR

Additional living superweapon deployment authorized.

### ESCALATION V — EXTINCTION RESPONSE

Enemy gets access to weapons specifically designed to kill your strongest unit.

That's much better than:

> You killed civilians. −10 morality.

You killed civilians?

**Congratulations. You just convinced the enemy command to deploy something terrifying.**

The consequence is gameplay.

# 6. Your "Protect the Scientist" mode

This should absolutely exist, but I'd rename it:

# BLACK SITE

One faction secretly knows where **Dr. Elena Voss** is located.

The defending team sees:

**PROTECT VOSS**

Attackers see:

**LOCATE VOSS**

The map contains perhaps:

**20–30 compounds/houses.**

But here's where it gets interesting.

Attackers don't just search houses randomly.

They gather **intelligence**.

Potential intelligence sources:

* civilians
* captured soldiers
* computers
* phone records
* vehicles
* surveillance towers
* radio intercepts
* witnesses

So attackers progressively narrow the search area.

Initially:

**SEARCH AREA: 6 km²**

Intel discovered:

**3 km²**

More intelligence:

**800 m**

Eventually:

**3 possible buildings**

Now the defenders realize:

> Shit. They're getting close.

# 7. Interrogation

Don't turn this into a dialogue minigame.

Keep it tactical.

Approach NPC.

**INTERROGATE — HOLD E — 8 seconds**

You become vulnerable.

Possible information:

> "Military trucks came through yesterday."

Map updates.

Or:

> "I saw soldiers near the water tower."

Or:

> "I don't know anything."

And civilians shouldn't magically know whether they're "good or bad." They know **pieces of information** based on what they've plausibly observed.

Much stronger simulation.

---

# 8. Killing civilians versus interrogation

Now your escalation system becomes brilliant.

Player can interrogate:

**8 seconds**

Or kill/search:

**2 seconds**

So brutality becomes **faster**.

That's important.

You aren't lecturing players.

You're tempting them.

Fast strategy:

> Kick doors, kill everyone, search everything.

Slow strategy:

> Interrogate, investigate, gather intelligence.

But brutality accelerates **enemy escalation**.

That's an actual strategic decision.

---

# 9. Why attackers can't simply destroy every house

This is exactly the loophole you identified.

There should be several reasons.

First, destroying a building may destroy the scientist, causing mission failure.

Second, it destroys intelligence sources.

Third, civilian casualties increase escalation.

Fourth, indiscriminate destruction reveals attacker positions.

Fifth, heavy weapons require authorization/ammunition.

So you CAN level the neighborhood.

The game doesn't put up an invisible wall.

It says:

> Fine.

Then makes you live with the military consequences.

That's far better game design.

---

# 10. Buildings and destruction

Don't start with fully simulated skyscraper destruction.

That will eat your project alive.

Use **destruction tiers**.

### Tier 0 — Static

Mountains, terrain, major infrastructure.

### Tier 1 — Props

Walls, fences, doors, glass, trees.

Fully destructible.

### Tier 2 — Tactical structures

Small houses, checkpoints, towers.

Use predefined destruction states:

`INTACT → DAMAGED → BREACHED → COLLAPSED`

### Tier 3 — Major structures

Bases, cloning facilities, research centers.

Destroy components rather than entire geometry.

Power generator.

Radar.

AA battery.

Clone chamber.

Communications.

Vehicle depot.

That gets you **80% of the tactical value of destruction without building a physics nightmare.**

---

# 11. What happens if you're inside a collapsing building?

Don't simulate every brick.

Trigger a structural collapse state.

Player receives:

**STRUCTURAL FAILURE**

3-second warning if appropriate.

Then depending upon character capability:

Normal human:

Likely incapacitated/killed.

Armored soldier:

Heavy damage.

Superhuman:

Damage based on durability.

Flying character:

Can escape through openings.

Extreme superweapon:

Might literally burst through the collapsing structure.

That makes powers meaningful without requiring structural engineering simulation.

# 12. Game modes

Now we can build modes that exploit the systems instead of generic Deathmatch.

### BLACK SITE

Find/protect the scientist.

### GENOME

Kill enemy superweapon → recover biological material → extract it.

### CLONE WAR

Destroy the enemy cloning infrastructure before your reserve reaches zero.

### BROKEN ARROW

A catastrophic experimental weapon crashes somewhere on a huge map. Both factions race to locate and recover it.

### HUNT

One player controls an extremely powerful living superweapon.

Enemy team gets infantry, vehicles, aircraft and progressively stronger anti-super weapons.

Think **monster hunt + military operation**.

### EXTRACTION

Capture an enemy living superweapon **alive**.

Killing them fails or severely reduces the reward.

Now tranquilizers, restraints, EMPs, suppression weapons and transport vehicles matter.

### CONVOY

Transport genetic material/scientist/superweapon across perhaps 10 km.

Attackers choose where to ambush.

Defenders choose route.

Air reconnaissance becomes important.

### AIR SUPERIORITY

Jets + flying characters fight for control of airspace while ground teams capture radar/AA installations.

### SIEGE

One faction has a fortified mountain installation.

Attackers have combined arms.

Defenders have:

AA
artillery
bunkers
radar
superweapon

### INSURGENCY

One faction possesses superior military power.

Other faction blends into civilian settlements.

Finding combatants becomes part of the problem.

Escalation becomes extremely important.

### LAST WEAPON

Both factions begin without superweapons.

There is **one dormant living weapon somewhere on the map.**

Find it.

Wake it.

Convince/control/capture it.

Now the entire balance of the match changes.

# 13. One mode I think could become Power World's signature

## GODFALL

Each team possesses:

**1 Living Superweapon
40–100 clones
vehicles
aircraft
research facility**

You win by destroying the enemy cloning core.

But here's the twist.

Superweapons are enormously powerful.

When one dies:

**its corpse becomes the most valuable object on the battlefield.**

Enemy steals genome.

Research begins.

Enemy soldiers start acquiring derivative powers.

Therefore every time you deploy your god-tier character you're making a strategic wager:

> Can I exploit this power without allowing the enemy to acquire it?

Now imagine your Superman-equivalent gets shot down **behind enemy lines**.

Command immediately announces:

> **WEAPON DOWN. GENETIC COMPROMISE IMMINENT.**

You have maybe 4 minutes.

Friendly commander has choices:

**RECOVER**
Send helicopters.

**SECURE**
Send infantry.

**DENY**
Call artillery.

**STERILIZE**
Send bomber.

Meanwhile the enemy is desperately trying to extract a sample.

That's a game.

# 15. Your anti-aircraft idea becomes critical

A flying superhero should be terrifying.

But the ground shouldn't be helpless.

Build a hierarchy.

**Rifle**

Almost useless against extreme supers.

**Heavy machine gun**

Useful against low-level flyers.

**Autocannon**

Dangerous.

**Radar-guided AA**

Very dangerous.

**SAM**

Potentially lethal.

**Experimental anti-super cannon**

Extremely dangerous but stationary and slow.

Now Superman-level player sees:

**MISSILE LOCK**

They need to:

dodge
terrain-mask
destroy radar
attack SAM site
send ground forces
use countermeasures

Suddenly your flying mechanic has strategic depth.

# 16. Power levels should be asymmetric

I would actually classify characters.

**Tier 0 — Human**

Soldiers, snipers, pilots.

**Tier 1 — Enhanced**

Captain-America-ish.

**Tier 2 — Tactical Superhuman**

Spider-Man-ish.

**Tier 3 — Heavy Superhuman**

Iron-Man-ish.

**Tier 4 — Strategic Weapon**

Superman-ish.

**Tier 5 — Catastrophic Weapon**

Goku-ish.

Tier 5 shouldn't just have:

`50,000 HP`

Their existence should change the match.

Radar detects them.

Command announces deployment.

Enemy gets emergency capabilities.

Something like:

> **STRATEGIC ENERGY EVENT DETECTED**

That should make everybody on the battlefield look up.

# 17. The questions we need to answer before you build more content

This is where I would stop adding random weapons and start locking your design.

1. **How many human players are you targeting per match?** 8? 16? 32? 64?

2. Are the clone soldiers **players, AI, or both?**

3. Can one player command AI clone squads?

4. Does every player select a hero, or are superweapons scarce?

5. Who controls aircraft? Players exclusively or AI too?

6. Can superweapons pilot vehicles, or would that be pointless?

7. Can a Superman-tier character pick up vehicles?

8. Can they throw vehicles?

9. Can they grab another flying player?

10. Can they carry soldiers?

11. Can players be captured instead of killed?

12. How long should a normal match last: 15, 30, 45, 60 minutes?

13. Is there persistent progression between matches?

14. Is genome research match-specific or persistent?

15. Can factions create custom hybrid soldiers from multiple genomes?

16. Can enhancements fail or mutate?

17. Can the enemy steal research rather than stealing a corpse?

18. Can the cloning facility itself be infiltrated?

19. Can enemy players disguise themselves as clones?

20. Can you capture an enemy cloning facility?

21. What happens when your clone reserve hits zero?

22. Does the player then permanently die for that match?

23. Can clones replenish through resources?

24. Are civilians simulated continuously or spawned around objectives?

25. Do civilians remember what they witnessed?

26. Can civilians flee and report your position?

27. Can civilians lie during interrogation?

28. Can they support one faction?

29. Can destroying civilian infrastructure create refugees?

30. Does escalation affect only military reinforcements, or the world itself?

31. Can both sides become equally brutal?

32. Are there neutral factions?

33. Are there police/local military forces separate from both teams?

34. How much terrain can eye beams penetrate?

35. Can beams cut through vehicles?

36. Can powers destroy terrain?

37. Can a Goku-tier blast permanently alter the battlefield?

38. What exactly counts as cover against a superweapon?

39. How do ordinary soldiers detect extremely fast characters?

40. Does radar detect biological flyers?

41. Does infrared?

42. Can supers hide their energy signature?

43. What happens when a sniper shoots a superweapon in the eye?

44. Are powers governed by **energy**, cooldowns, stamina, or combinations?

45. Can soldiers develop countermeasures after studying a power?

46. Can powers interact—beam vs beam, shield vs missile, electricity vs vehicle, etc.?

47. Can superweapons defect?

48. Are they people with agency or literally government-owned biological weapons?

49. Why are these factions fighting?

50. **Most important:** what can an ordinary rifleman accomplish that your strongest superweapon cannot?

That last answer determines whether **Power World becomes a genuinely interesting combined-arms game or just a superhero sandbox with tanks sitting around.**

The strongest architecture I see is **Battlefield/Operation Flashpoint scale + Bid For Power mobility/combat + asymmetric superweapons + clone attrition + genome warfare + systemic escalation**. Three.js is capable of supporting the concept, but the simulation should stay separate from the Three.js scene graph so these rules don't become tangled into your rendering code.

And I would build **GODFALL on RED VALLEY first**. One map, one complete mode, one clone loop, one superweapon corpse/genome loop, infantry + one tank + one helicopter + one AA emplacement. Don't build 15 modes yet. If that combination produces memorable battles, everything else can grow from it.
