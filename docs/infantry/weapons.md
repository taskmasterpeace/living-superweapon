Research file for **War World** (`D:/git/ShootEM`). What Infantry Online (Harmless Games /
Sony Online Entertainment, 1999–2012; community-run as Free Infantry) actually did with weapons,
items, ammo, classes, deployables, vehicles and progression — with real numbers where they survive.

## 1. THE ITEM SYSTEM — **CONFIRMED**

### 1.1 A zone is a folder of data files

A zone server loads a set of asset files; the server emulator's own README enumerates the
extensions it requires: **`.blo` `.cfg` `.itm` `.lio` `.lvl` `.nws` `.rpg` `.veh`** [SRV].
Relevant here:

- **`.itm`** — the item file. Every weapon, every piece of armour, every grenade, every medkit.
- **`.veh`** — the vehicle file.
- **`.cfg`** — the zone config (game rules, level, help menu).
- **`.lio`** — level items/objects (flags, spawn points, warps).

Weapons were **not code**. A weapon was a row in a CSV-like item file. SOE released the editing
tools publicly in 2007 so players could build their own zones and submit them for monthly public
testing [Massively OP, [massivelyop.com/2018/10/06](https://massivelyop.com/2018/10/06/1999s-infantry-online-has-been-revived-and-restored-by-its-community/)];
the tool for the config was the **Infantry CFG Editor ("ICE")**, and companion tools included
**BlobEdit** (`.blo`/`.lvb`) and **CFSConvW** (sprite conversion) [[freeinfantry.com forum, "Infantry Editors"](http://www.freeinfantry.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=7&t=47)].

### 1.2 There are ELEVEN item classes, not one — **CONFIRMED**

`Docs/Itm/` in the server repo documents each item class as a numbered element list [SRV]:

| Item class | Doc file | What it models |
|---|---|---|
| **Projectile** | `Projectile.txt` | Anything that fires a thing — guns, launchers, grenades, mines |
| **Utility** | `Utility.txt` | Armour, shields, cloaks, batteries, sensors — the "worn" layer |
| **Ammo** | `AmmoItem.txt` | Pure consumable stock |
| **Repair** | `Repair.txt` | Medkits, engineer repair, healing |
| **Multi-Use** | `Multi-Use Items.txt` | One item with several firing modes |
| **MultiItem** | `MultiItem.txt` | A bundle that grants cash/energy/health/repair/experience + up to 17 item slots |
| **Item Maker** | `Item Maker.txt` | An item that *spawns another item* in the world — deployables |
| **Vehicle Maker** | `Vehicle Maker.txt` | An item that *spawns a vehicle* — turret kits, vehicle kits |
| **Skill** | `Skills.txt` | The class/skill purchase — grants up to **16 skills**, 2 elements each |
| **Control** | `Control.txt` | Takes control of another unit |
| **Upgrade** | `Upgrade.txt` | **15 Input-Item → Output-Item pairs** — an in-place transform |

⚠ **This taxonomy is the real lesson, and it is the thing War World is most likely to under-build.**
A turret is not a special case in Infantry — it is a *Vehicle Maker* item. A medkit is not a special
case — it is a *Repair* item. A class is not a special case — it is a *Skill* item you **buy with
money from the same shop as bullets**. There is one purchase verb and one inventory.

> War World's generated families should be using.

### 1.4 Six damage channels, renamed by the zone — **LIKELY**

The engine names its six channels **Kinetic, Explosive, Electronic, Psionic, Bypass, Energy** in a
fixed order — identical in the projectile damage block (152–175) and the armour resist block
(53–64 of `Utility.txt`) [SRV]. The live zone dump prints six channels **in the same order** but
under different names: **Kinetic, Explosive, Plasma, Gas/Chemical, Armor Piercing, Energy Drain**
[WDB]. The slots line up one-for-one.

Reading: **the engine ships six fixed damage channels and the zone gives them meaning.** Channel 5
being "Bypass" in the engine and "Armor Piercing" in the zone is the tell — the engine's name is
mechanical ("skip the armour step"), the zone's is fictional. Marked LIKELY because I did not find
the config key that does the renaming.

The dump's output format for one weapon, quoted whole [WDB]:

```

	Jump Trooper OR Infantry

somewhat slower round. Expensive.
Weight:                       8.500 kg | Price:                           12500
Sub-Weapon Count:                    2 |
Max Allowed(Soft):                   1 |
Fire Delay:                     100 ms | Reload Delay:                  1200 ms
Ammo Capacity:                       3 | Ammo Type:                  Ammo Rifle
Ammo Per Shot                        1 | Energy Cost                    0.00 kJ
Fire Angle:                        0 d | Friction:                      0.00%/s
Muzzle Velocity:             14.52 m/s | Lifetime:                       3.00 s
Wall Bounces:                        0 | Floor Bounces:                       0
Proximity Radius:               0.00 m | Ballistic:                          No

Damage Type                  Inner             Outer      Blast Radius
Kinetic                      20.00             15.00            0.15 m
Explosive                     0.00              0.00            0.00 m
Plasma                        0.00              0.00            0.00 m
Gas/Chemical                  0.00              0.00            0.00 m
Armor Piercing                0.00              0.00            0.00 m
Energy Drain                120.00            100.00            0.15 m
```

Note what a plain assault rifle carries: **20 kinetic damage and 120 energy drain**. Energy drain is
6× the HP damage. That is not decoration — see §3.4.

**Field notes:**
- **`Inner` / `Outer` / `Blast Radius`** — every hit is an area query. `Inner` is the direct-hit
  figure, `Outer` the edge figure, and damage falls off between them across `Blast Radius`. A rifle
  bullet has a 0.15 m blast radius; it is a tiny explosion. **There is no hitscan in this game.**
- **`Sub-Weapon Count`** = projectiles emitted per trigger pull — **LIKELY**. Evidence: shotguns
  carry 10 (`SiG Arms m2 AS`, `Maklov G2 ACW`) and the armour page notes it tracks *"pellets in
  groups of 4, approximating half a shotgun blast"* [ARM]; the `Micro Missile Launcher` carries 6
  with a 1-round clip. It is 2 on most rifles, which I cannot cleanly explain.
- **`Max Allowed (Soft)` vs `(Hard)`** — two distinct caps in the dump. Soft appears on primaries
  you carry one of; Hard on consumables (`Ammo Rifle` Hard 400) and on things with a live-instance
  rule (`AP Mine` Hard 5). **LIKELY**: soft = purchase guidance, hard = engine refusal.
- **`Ballistic: No`** on a bullet weapon — so `Ballistic` is *arcing/gravity-affected*, not
  "is a firearm". Grenades and the GL are the arcing ones.

### 1.6 The real file, decoded — **CONFIRMED**

I retrieved `Complete Zones/ctfpl/ctfpl.itm` — **the actual item file of the actual CTFPL zone the
fan site documented** [ITM]. It closes almost every open question about the format.

**It is a plain CSV.** First row, verbatim:
```
13,v57,45, "IDF Generator","Armor","!(24|4|%4)","The Inertial Dampening Field Generator creates a
barrier around the user that has startling effects upon projectiles.  It either slows them down to
speeds where they do not cause damage, or stops them outright. ...
```
Mapping straight onto `Docs/Itm/*.txt`: `13` = Class · `v57` = Version · `45` = ID ·
`"IDF Generator"` = Name · `"Armor"` = Category · `"!(24|4|%4)"` = **Skill Logic** · then Description.

**492 item rows. The class ID selects the schema, and the field counts match the docs exactly:**

| Class ID | Rows | Fields | Matches doc | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **6** | 261 | **227** | `Projectile.txt` (227 elements) ✓ | `Maklov AR mk 606-MAIN`, `AP Mine` |
| **8** | 69 | 109 | Multi-Use | `Maklov AR mk 606`, `Pulsar`, `Rocket Turret` |
| **13** | 36 | **137** | `Utility.txt` (137 elements) ✓ | `IDF Generator`, `Carapace`, `Shock Armor` |
| **16** | 27 | **63** | `Skills.txt` (63 elements) ✓ | `Infantry`, `Medic`, `Heavy`, `Observer` |
| **4** | 15 | **31** | `AmmoItem.txt` (31 positions) ✓ | `Ammo Rifle`, `Ammo MG`, `Rocket` |
| 1 / 15 / 7 / 17 / 11 / 14 / 12 | 19/18/16/13/9/8/1 | varies | — | vehicles, teleports, `Medikit`, `AutoGun MG Kit`, `Reprogramming Kit` |

⚠ **A weapon is TWO items.** `Maklov AR mk 606` is a **class-8 Multi-Use item** (the thing you buy);
`Maklov AR mk 606-MAIN` is a **class-6 Projectile** (the thing that fires). The Multi-Use item
references projectiles as its firing modes — which is what `Multi-Use Items.txt` is for, and how a
weapon gets alt-fire without a second weapon slot. **This is the single cleanest idea in the file:
the shop entry and the ballistics are different objects.**

**Unit scales, recovered by diffing raw values against the dump's printed figures:**

| Quantity | Raw | Printed | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight | `10000` (Carapace) | 10.000 kg | **÷1000** |
| Buy Price | `18000` | $18,000 | **1:1** |
| Damage inner/outer | `45000` (g9 Sniper) | 45.00 | **÷1000** |
| Armour `Ignore` | `8000` (Carapace kinetic) | 8.000 | **÷1000** |
| Fire Delay | `100` (AP Mine) | 1000 ms | **×10 ms** |
| Reload | `250` (AP Mine) | 2500 ms | **×10 ms** |
| Alive Time | `15000` (AP Mine) | 150.00 s | **×10 ms** |
| Muzzle Velocity | `4000` AR / `7000` g9 | 14.52 / 25.40 m/s | ÷≈275.6 |
| Blast radius | `4` AR / `80` AP Mine | 0.15 / 2.90 m | ÷≈27.6 |

⚠ **All timing is stored in 10 ms ticks** — one consistent unit across fire delay, reload and
projectile lifetime. The metre figures on the fan site are a *conversion the site's author applied*
(both velocity and radius divide by a constant), so **"metres" is not a native Infantry unit.**

`Sell Price` of `-1` means **not sellable** (seen on `AP Mine`, `Maklov g9 Sniper`) [ITM].

### 1.7 Skill Logic is a real expression language — **CONFIRMED (operators) / UNVERIFIED (`%`)**

Decoding the raw strings against the fan site's English proves the grammar:

| Item | Raw [ITM] | Fan-site English [WDB] |
|---|---|---|
| `AutoGun MG Kit` | `6` | Combat Engineer |
| `Teleport Beacon` | `1\|6\|7` | Medic OR CEng OR SL |
| `Titan Arms 9mm Carbine` | `!(5\|4\|15)` | NOT Heavy Weapons AND NOT Infiltrator |
| `Frag Grenade` | `!(4\|%4)` | NOT Infiltrator |
| `Cloaking Unit` | `4\|10&!%4` | Infiltrator |
| `AP Mine` | `%3\|6\|2\|3\|5\|7\|20` | CEng OR JT OR Inf OR HW OR SL |

**Grammar: `|` = OR · `&` = AND · `!` = NOT · `()` = grouping · bare integer = skill ID.**
Note `!(5|4|15)` renders in English as *"NOT Heavy Weapons AND NOT Infiltrator"* — De Morgan's law,
applied by whatever generated the English. The engine stores the compact form.

**Skill ID map**, recovered from the 27 class-16 rows [ITM]:
```
1 Field Medic   5 Heavy Weapons   9 Duel      12 Captain   15 Zombie        19 Marauder
2 Jump Trooper  6 Combat Engineer 10 SciOps   13 BioChem   16 DuelerSeeHP   20 AT
3 Infantry      7 Squad Leader    11 Sarge    14 Marine    17 Observer
4 Infiltrator   8 Staff
```
⚠ **UNVERIFIED: the `%` prefix.** `%4` appears alongside plain `4`, and `!%4` guards most armour.
It is a second class of test — plausibly "currently operating as" versus "owns the skill" — but I
could not confirm it. Do not copy `%` semantics on the strength of this file alone.

Also visible: **CTFPL's class-16 skill items are all `Buy Price 0`, `Weight 0`, category `Staff`**,
each granting exactly one skill and guarded by `!N` (you cannot buy the class you already are)
[ITM] — consistent with a league zone that hands out classes free (§3.6).

⚠ **`ctfpl.itm` is version `v57` and is NOT the build the fan site dumped.** Cross-checking:
`Maklov g9 Sniper` reads Buy Price **0** here against the site's **$8,500**; the AR's kinetic inner
is `22000` (22.0) against the site's 20.00; its fire delay is `15` ticks (150 ms) against 100 ms.
**The file confirms the format, the schema and the scales absolutely — its values are a later
revision.** Where §2's tables and this section disagree, §2 is the older snapshot.

### 2.1 The brand system — **CONFIRMED**

Infantry's armoury is **manufacturer × role**, exactly the axis War World already generates on.
Brands appearing across multiple roles [WDB]: **Maklov** (18 items), **Kuchler** (12),
**Titan Arms** (9), **Kamenev** (5), **Steiner** (4), **Unittech** (5), **SiG Arms** (1).

The same role recurs across brands with *different numbers*, and the flavour text says why —
`Kuchler PC v2`: *"Kuchler makes a higher quality PC, and uses special materials for shielding
that are lighter than normal PC's. Weighs a bit less but costs more."* [WDB] — and it does:
18.0 kg / $16,500 against `Maklov XVI PC2000` at 22.0 kg / $10,000, **identical damage**.
**That is a pure weight-for-money sidegrade with no stat advantage at all.**

### 3.2 Weight is a soft/hard band, and the penalty is graduated — **CONFIRMED (formula derived)**

`Utility.txt` carries two weight fields per suit: **Element 132 `Normal Weight`, Element 133
`Stop Weight`** [SRV]. Per class [CLS]:

| Class | Weight limit |
|---|---|
| Infiltrator | **30** kg soft (50 hard) |
| Jump Trooper | 40 soft (50 hard) |
| Infantryman | 45 soft (55 hard) |
| Field Medic | 50 soft (60 hard) |
| Squad Leader | 50 soft (60 hard) |
| Heavy Weapons | **60** soft (**80** hard) |
| Combat Engineer | 60 soft (80 hard) |

The loadout page prints an **"% overweight"** figure per build [LOD]. I tested the obvious formula
against all ten published loadouts:

```
overweight% = (total − soft) / (hard − soft) × 100
```

**Nine of ten reproduce exactly** (Medic 50.20 kg → 2%; Engineer 61.95 kg → 9.75% ≈ 10%; the seven
under-limit builds → 0%). The tenth (Heavy Weapons 60.10 kg) predicts 0.5% and the page prints 0 —
a rounding difference. **Marked CONFIRMED-by-reconstruction.** So the soft limit is not a wall: it
is the start of a band, and you can trade speed for firepower continuously across it until the hard
limit stops you dead.

**The encumbrance rule, stated outright by the official manual** [MAN], verbatim:

> **ENCUMBERANCE**
> Put simply - the more you carry the slower you move.
> Each player can carry upto 15kg before it starts to affect his speed. Once you reach 30Kg then you
> will be unable to move. (Later this will be based off the players Strength attribute).

⚠ **AND VEHICLES CARRY THEIR OWN ENCUMBRANCE BUDGET.** This is the best single design idea in the
whole logistics layer [MAN], verbatim:

> -The M1 jetpack can allow the player to carry 30Kg before it slows down.
> -The Hoverboard starts to slow down when the player is carrying only 10kg
> Thus the jetpack is great for people who want to be human tanks but the hoverboard is best suited
> for those who want to be a fast lightly armed skirmisher.

**The vehicle does not change your speed — it changes the weight at which you start losing speed.**
So a vehicle choice is a *loadout* choice, retroactively repricing every item you were considering.

Changelog support [REL]: v0.25 `"encumbrance system and related items"` · v0.29 `"encumbrance guage
added to notepad"` · v0.36 `"weight/encumbrance display updating constantly"` · v0.39 `"attribute
relationships established: health, energy, thrust, top-speed, weight"` · v0.58 attribute-based
carrying adjustments applying to `"default vehicles that specify carrying capacity explicitly"`.

### 3.3b Where the money came from — **CONFIRMED**

Four income channels, all zone-tunable:

1. **BOUNTY on kills.** [MAN], verbatim: *"Your bounty is the amount of points someone gets for
   killing you. your bounty goes up gradually the longer you stay alive and increases every time you
   get a kill."* ⚠ **This is a self-balancing bounty system** — the player who is winning is
   literally worth more money to kill, so the economy pushes the game back toward parity without any
   explicit rubber-banding. **Real number:** [REL] v0.71 `"capped maximum bounty possible at 30000"`.
   **Assists paid too** [MAN]: *"If you are near an enemy when he is killed by a teamate you get a
   small amount of points as an 'assist'."* Anti-farming shipped later — [REL] v1.28 `"added ability
   to not reward players who kill the same player twice in a certain period of time"`.
2. **Objectives** — [REL] v0.44 fixed `"flag victory points/cash"`; jackpot systems in v1.05/v1.13.
3. **Periodic income scaled by the ground you hold** — [REL] v0.57 `"periodic reward enhanced to obey
   terrain reward percentages"` and `"added ability for level designers to control bounty and reward
   on a per terrain type basis"`. ⚠ **Holding better terrain pays better.** Map control *is* income.
4. **A flat login grant** in wipe-on-login zones (§3.6).

### 3.5 Armour is flat-ignore **plus** percentage — **CONFIRMED**

`Utility.txt` gives each suit an `Ignore` and a `Percent` per damage channel (elements 53–64) [SRV].
Live example, `Carapace` [WDB]:

```

Kinetic                      8.000            20.0 %
Explosive                    5.000            20.0 %
Plasma                       0.000             0.0 %
Gas/Chemical                 0.000             0.0 %
Armor Piercing               0.000             0.0 %
Energy Drain                 0.000             0.0 %
```

`Ignore 8` against Kinetic is why the 4-damage shotgun pellet does literally nothing — **the flat
subtraction happens first, and it is larger than the whole pellet.** And Carapace has **0/0 against
Plasma, Gas, AP and Energy Drain** — the heaviest armour in the game is naked to four of six damage
channels. That is what makes a Plasma Cannon worth 18 kg.

Armour costs weight and money: Ceramax $2,000/2 kg → Kevlite $8,000/4 kg → CMP4 $8,000/6 kg →

are separate items again: `PF Generator` and `IDF Generator` both $10,000/2–3 kg, and batteries
stack on top — `Light/Medium/Heavy PowerCell` at $100/$500/$1,000 for 2/5/10 kg [WDB]. The armour
page shows a Heavy PowerCell moving a build from 500 → **750** total energy [ARM].

### 3.6 The zone decides whether money matters at all — **CONFIRMED**

⚠ So the economy was a **zone-level dial**, not a game-level law. A competitive zone handed out

from a persistence system into a *within-match* resource. Persistent zones existed alongside it.

### 4.1 Seven classes

**Infantryman · Heavy Weapons · Jump Trooper · Infiltrator · Combat Engineer · Field Medic ·
Squad Leader** [CLS]. Stats in §3.4, weight in §3.2. Roles [CLS], condensed:

- **Heavy Weapons** — autocannons, gauss, plasma cannons. Slowest; heavy weapons have a **minimum

### 4.1b A class IS a skill, and a skill IS a body — **CONFIRMED**

There is no class-picker. You **buy a Skill item**, and buying it makes you that class, because
**the skill carries a default vehicle and your on-foot soldier is that vehicle** [REL]:

- v0.38 `"use of vehicles and items tied to skill logic qualifications (the great circle is complete)"`
- v0.40 `"ability to tie default vehicle to a skill that you own added"` and `"predefined characters added to skills"`
- v0.43 fixed `"skill-based default vehicle was not appearing when you first log in, only when you buy the skill"`
- v0.89 `"enhanced skill logic to support vehicle operating class qualifications"`

⚠ **That is the load-bearing unification.** Skill → default vehicle → your body. Class abilities are
just the vehicle's stats; class gear access is just the item logic passing. Nothing in the engine
knows what a "class" is.

Purchased at the **skill screen** (`skills` terrain type, v0.39, or **F11**, v0.55). Skills could be
**unpurchaseable** (v0.45), **picked up off the map** as objects (v0.45, v0.49), **team-allocated**
(v0.71), **gated on XP** (v0.80 `"ability to have skill logic dependent on points and experience"`),
and buying one could be configured to **wipe your attributes and inventory** (v0.80 `"ability to
selectively reset attribs/skills on skill purchase"`) — i.e. switching class could cost you your kit.

Gating was shown honestly in the UI: v0.43 `"only non-skill-qualified or non-vehicle-qualified items
appear in the darker color"`; v0.40 `"skill qualification for utility items is now noted when that is
the reason you can't use something"`.

### 4.2 Access is a boolean expression, not a class table — **CONFIRMED**

Every item carries **Element 6: `Skill Logic : String`** [SRV], and the dump prints it as
`Required skills:` [WDB]. **36 distinct expressions** appear across the 156 items. Real examples:

```
None
Infantry
Heavy Weapons
Jump Trooper OR Infantry
Infiltrator OR Squad Leader
NOT Infiltrator
NOT Heavy Weapons
NOT Heavy Weapons AND NOT Infiltrator
Combat Engineer OR Jump Trooper OR Infantry OR Heavy Weapons OR Squad Leader
Field Medic OR Jump Trooper OR Infantry OR Combat Engineer OR Squad Leader
```

⚠ **`NOT` is the interesting operator and War World has no analogue.** `Titan Arms 9mm Carbine`
requires *"NOT Heavy Weapons AND NOT Infiltrator"* — it is a weapon defined by who **can't** have it.
`Frag Grenade` is `NOT Infiltrator`: everyone gets grenades except the sneak. That is a far cheaper
way to author 156 items than a per-class allow-list, and it degrades gracefully when you add a class.

Distribution across the 156 items [WDB]: `None` 38 · `Heavy Weapons` 19 · `Combat Engineer` 13 ·
`NOT Infiltrator` 9 · `Jump Trooper` 9 · `Field Medic` 9 · `Infiltrator` 8 · `Infantry` 5, remainder
in multi-class disjunctions.

A class itself is a **Skill item** you buy: `Skills.txt` gives it Buy Price, Weight, Max Allowed and
then **16 skill slots, each a `Skill N Logic : String` + `Skill N ID : Integer` pair** [SRV].

### 4.3 Loadouts are client-side macro files — **CONFIRMED**

> *"To use any of these loadouts, right click on the appropriate hyper-link and save the file to your
> Infantry directory."* [LOD]

⚠ **The loadout system IS the chat-macro system.** `?buy` is typed into chat, so storing a chat macro
stores a loadout. [PLY], verbatim: *"To store it as a macro instead of saying it, hit SHIFT + F1 (or
F2, etc.) instead of ENTER… **You are given the ability to store up to 24 macros.**"* — and
*"While this is useful to saying things, it can also be used to 'say' prompt commands. Almost all
players use this to buy ammunition with the '?buy' command."* **One feature, two jobs, zero extra UI.**

Macros 1–12 in every shipped file are comms barks with **live substitution tokens** —
`%coord`, `%heading`, `%flagcount`, `%flagdrop`, `%selfname`, `%count[Tsolvy Crystals]` [LOD]. Real
line from the medic's file: `'==== Medic @ Home Base! [ %coord, %count[Tsolvy Crystals] Tsolvy ]`
(a leading `'` makes it team-only [MAN]). ⚠ **A macro can report your own inventory count** — that is
how the squad tracked the miner's ore without a UI for it.

Mid-game rearrangement needed no purchase [MAN]: right-click an inventory item to assign it to the
**right hand (CTRL), left hand (TAB), or six belt slots on F5–F10**; SPACE picks up off the ground;
a weapon with no ammo is highlighted red. [REL] v0.40 saved `"slot assignments on a per zone basis
between program runs"`.

The squad shipped **numbered macro slots**, and the convention was explicit [LOD]:

> *"The 13th macro will buy all permanent gear for you (except Enhanced Sensors which you must buy
> manually if you need them). The 14th macro will buy all ammunition for you assuming you do have
> Enhanced Sensors. The 15th macro will buy all ammunition for you assuming you do not have Enhanced
> Sensors."*

So: **one key rebuys your whole kit, another rebuys only consumables.** Macros also carried team
comms with optional sounds, deliberately rationed [PLY]: *"the macros were deliberately set to that
only a few have sounds... If you hear the sound 'acknowledge!', it is damn important."* There were
also `?drop` macros for handing off mined minerals and kits.

## 6. VEHICLES — **PARTIAL / LIKELY**

### 6.1 Five vehicle classes in the engine — **CONFIRMED**

`Docs/Veh/` documents: **`Car.txt` · `Computer.txt` · `Dependent.txt` · `Shared.txt` ·
`Spectator.txt`** [SRV].

⚠ **The class names carry the architecture**, and this is a strong reading (**LIKELY**):
- **Car** — a driven vehicle.
- **Shared** — a vehicle holding **multiple players**.
- **Dependent** — a vehicle *attached to a parent* — i.e. a turret seat on another vehicle.
- **Computer** — AI-operated. **This is what an AutoGun is.** A built turret is not a special

- **Spectator** — the observer camera, modelled as a vehicle.

## 7. PROGRESSION — **CONFIRMED (structure) / UNVERIFIED (numbers)**

### 7.1 Three parallel currencies: cash, experience, rank

[REL] v0.99 (8 Jan 2001): `"added ?playerchart command that lists the vehicle/class player is
currently operating as well as their experience, rank, and cash."` Three tracked stats, one command.

- **Cash** — spent at the store (§3.3). Grantable by admins (`*cash`, v0.66).
- **Experience** — from v0.39 `"added reward system for experience and cash (currently configured to
  mirror point system, but that will change)"`; items could grant it directly (`MultiItem`
  `Position 36 : Experience` [SRV]; [REL] v0.83 `"added multi-items and support for experience
  pickups"`); v1.27 fixed XP/cash going negative.
- **Rank** — ⚠ **not a system, just a label.** [REL] v0.81, verbatim: `"added ranking type system (up
  to level designer to make use of it, it's really little more than a text representation of your
  experience points level)"`.
- **Attributes** were a fourth axis, bought on a *different price curve*: [REL] v0.39 `"attribute
  relationships established: health, energy, thrust, top-speed, weight"` and `"different pricing
  schemes added for attributes and skills"`.

### 7.2 Weapons are gated by SKILL + CASH, not by level

Every one of the 156/492 items carries a Buy Price and a Skill Logic string, and **nothing in any
item schema carries a level or rank requirement** [SRV][WDB][ITM]. The normal ladder is economic —
[MMO]: *"You start with the basic essentials and work your way to better, more costly armaments. By
destroying enemies, you earn the funds you need to progress to the top of the ladder."*

But a zone designer had **four** unlock currencies available if they wanted them:
cash · **XP/points** ([REL] v0.80 `"ability to have skill logic dependent on points and experience
added"`) · **map pickups** (v0.45 skills pickable off the level) · **team allocation** (v0.71).

### 7.3 Persistence is per-alias, per-zone — **CONFIRMED**

⚠ **This is the structural fact that makes the whole design work.** The character record is the
score record, and it is scoped to *(alias, zone)*:

- [REL] v0.95 `"fixed bug where permanent characters were getting reset as you changed arenas the
  first time you used an alias in a particular zone"`
- [REL] v1.00 `"Banners are kept track of by the server on a per-alias/per-zone basis."`
- [REL] v0.75 `"made squad-membership zone specific"` … `"squad-membership is now tied to the
  score-record, which means when scores are reset, the players squad membership will be dropped"`
- Player self-wipe: `?wipecharacter` (v0.39, spectator only); admin `*wipe` (v0.66).
- Not everything counted — v1.28: GravBall scores need ≥10 players, and `"private-arena scores are
  not kept on your permanent record either"`.

So **each zone is its own game with its own economy, roster and progression**, and one account walks
between them carrying nothing. That is why [MMO] can say *"Each time you exit the game, your progress
is saved so you can build your soldier for months"* while [PLY] says *"The CTFPL zone does not save
your characters"* — **both are true, of different zones.** The competitive league deliberately turned
persistence off so every match started level.

### 7.4 Business model — **CONFIRMED**

Free-to-play with limitations from launch; SOE added a **$6.95/month** tier in **May 2002**; **June
2007** SOE gave full free access; servers closed **March 2012**; community relaunch at
freeinfantry.com [WIK]. The modern revival is *"100% free with zero paid unlockables"*
([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2830720/FreeInfantry/)).

### 7.5 A trap in the fan sources

Squad sites show ranks like `[8F] SSG.` — these are **not** game progression. [42nd] roster page,
verbatim: *"The ranking merely reflects how many practice sessions a player has successfully
attended… It DOES NOT mean that the person has incredible dueling skills."*

## 9. WHAT WAR WORLD SHOULD STEAL

War World today: ~200 weapons as **families × brands × Mk tiers**, where a higher Mk hits harder but
the magazine shrinks and the reload drags. That is a real axis and Infantry validates it — but
Infantry got its depth from **four things War World does not have**, and only one of them is content.

### 9.1 STEAL FIRST — the two-resource body

**The single highest-value idea in this document.** Infantry gives every soldier **50–60 HP and
500–800 kJ**, and makes the battery do triple duty: it is your shield, it is the ammunition for
half the armoury, and it is your movement fuel. Every weapon carries *two* damage numbers —
20 kinetic **and** 120 energy drain on a plain assault rifle (§1.5).

Why it is worth more than any weapon you could add:
- It turns "which gun" into "which gun *against what*" without a single new stat.
- It makes **energy weapons vs ammo weapons** a genuine strategic fork: light and free but they eat
  your shield, or heavy and logistics-bound but they don't (§3.1).
- It gives you a whole weapon category — **guns that do zero HP damage** (Gravitron, Stunner,
  Electron Beamer, Disruptor). War World cannot currently express "this weapon is powerful and
  cannot kill anyone."

Concretely: give every War World weapon a second damage channel and a `energyCost` per shot. Mk
tiers then have a *third* axis to trade on, and "Mk V hits harder but reloads slower" becomes
"Mk V hits harder, reloads slower, **and drains your shield doing it**."

### 9.2 STEAL — Inner / Outer / Blast Radius on *every* weapon

Infantry has **no hitscan**. A rifle bullet is a 0.15 m explosion travelling at 14.52 m/s with a
3.00 s lifetime (§1.5). Everything is the same code path: bullet, grenade (4.65 m), Recoiless Rifle
(4.65 m), mine (2.90 m). One damage function, one falloff, and the difference between a rifle and a
howitzer is **three numbers**, not two systems.

This is the cheapest structural win available to War World: collapse your weapon damage into
`{inner, outer, radius}` and you get shotgun pellets, splash, mines and artillery from the same
model — and your generator can then interpolate across it, which it cannot do across special cases.

Related and nearly free: **muzzle velocity and lifetime are the real range stat.** The Maklov g9
Sniper is not defined by 45 damage; it is defined by 25.40 m/s against everyone else's 14.52, and
6.0 s of flight against 3.0 (§2.2). If War World's Mk tiers moved velocity and lifetime instead of
only damage, a Mk V would *feel* different at range rather than reading different on a tooltip.

### 9.3 STEAL — flat `Ignore` before percentage `Protect`

Two numbers per armour per damage channel (§3.5), and the flat one applies first. That one detail
produces the game's entire counter-structure for free:

- Carapace has `Kinetic Ignore 8`. A shotgun pellet does 4. **The pellet does nothing — ever.**
- Carapace has `0/0` against Plasma, Gas, AP and Energy Drain. The best armour in the game is naked
  to four of six channels.

⚠ **This is the mechanism War World most needs and is most likely to skip.** A pure-percentage
resist model can never produce "immune", only "resistant" — so you can never build a weapon that is
*the answer* to an armour, only one that is somewhat better. Infantry's flat-subtract-first gives
you hard immunity *and* hard counters as emergent consequences of two integers. LSW already learned
this lesson in its own damage-type work (*"a resistance must cut BOTH ways"*); this is the same rule
one level deeper.

### 9.4 STEAL — weight as a soft band, not a cap

`(total − soft) / (hard − soft)` — graduated penalty between two limits, hard stop at the top (§3.2),
verified against nine of ten published loadouts. Per class the soft limit runs 30 kg (Infiltrator) to
60 kg (Heavy Weapons / Engineer).

What it buys you: **every item becomes a decision against every other item**, permanently. The
Recoiless Rifle is 25 kg of a 60 kg allowance. Thirty rockets is another 10.5 kg. A hundred units of
Titanium Oxide is 20 kg and puts the engineer 10% overweight *on purpose* [LOD].

Right now War World's Mk tiers trade damage against magazine and reload — all *internal* to the
weapon. Weight makes the trade **external**: a Mk V that weighs more costs you a grenade, or your
armour, or your ammo. That is the single change that would make 200 weapons feel like 200 choices
rather than 200 rows.

### 9.5 STEAL — `Skill Logic` as a boolean string, including `NOT`

Element 6 on every item is a string like `Jump Trooper OR Infantry` or
`NOT Heavy Weapons AND NOT Infiltrator` (§4.2). 36 distinct expressions across 156 items.

Two reasons this beats a per-class allow-list:
1. **`NOT` lets you define a weapon by who can't have it.** `Frag Grenade` = `NOT Infiltrator`.
   One string, and every future class automatically gets grenades unless you say otherwise.
2. **It survives adding a class.** An allow-list needs editing 156 rows; a `NOT` expression needs
   editing zero.

For a *generated* armoury this matters more, not less: your generator can emit a skill expression as
readily as a damage number, and it will stay correct as the roster grows.

### 9.6 STEAL — expendables as a separate line

Every published loadout breaks out **permanent gear cost** from **expendables cost and weight** [LOD]
— because the second is what you pay again every death. Offensive Heavy Weapons: $62,700 total, of
which **$6,900 / 22.90 kg is expendable** (§3.3). And the macro convention follows it exactly: macro
13 rebuys everything, macro 14 rebuys only ammo (§4.3).

⚠ This is a **UI and economy insight, not a mechanic**, and it is cheap. If War World ever adds cost,
show the player these two numbers separately and give them one key for each. It converts a shop into
a logistics loop.

### 9.7 STEAL — the eleven item classes, especially `Vehicle Maker` and `Upgrade`

The taxonomy (§1.2) is the architectural lesson. Three specifics:

- **A turret is a `Vehicle Maker` item that spawns a `Computer`-class vehicle** (§5.1, §6.1). Not a
  building system. If War World ever wants deployables, this is how to get them without a second
  entity model.
- **`Turret Dismantle` is a weapon**, not a menu action (§5.2). Recovering your own structure costs a
  weapon slot and a trigger pull.
- **`Upgrade` is 15 Input-Item → Output-Item pairs** (§1.2). That is a whole crafting/Mk-upgrade
  system as a lookup table. **War World's Mk tiers are literally this shape already** — an Mk III
  that upgrades to an Mk IV is one input/output pair. Making it a real in-world item rather than a
  generation-time property would give the tier ladder a player-facing verb.

### 9.8 STEAL — the resource → fortification supply chain

Titanium Oxide is mined on **contested ground** by a designated player with a $10,000 vehicle, weighs
0.2 kg/unit, caps at 150, and **25 units are consumed per AutoGun** (§5.3). The medic's Tsolvy
Crystals work the same way: 1 per Medikit use, 5 per Deluxe.

⚠ **The load-bearing detail is that the resource has weight.** It is not an abstract counter — the
engineer is physically slowed by his own building supplies and the loadout page shows him accepting
10% overweight to carry them. That is what makes hauling ore a *decision* instead of an errand.

### 9.9 STEAL — one axis that is pure sidegrade, priced honestly

`Kuchler PC v2` vs `Maklov XVI PC2000`: **identical damage**, and the Kuchler is 18 kg / $16,500
against 22 kg / $10,000 (§2.1). You pay 65% more money to save 4 kg. Likewise the three rifle-calibre
ARs share damage, velocity and lifetime and differ *only* in weight, price and cadence (§2.2).

⚠ **This is the direct critique of War World's current ladder.** "Higher Mk hits harder but magazine
shrinks and reload drags" is a *vertical* trade — there is a right answer once you know the fight.
Infantry's brand axis is *horizontal*: Maklov is heavy/accurate/expensive, Titan Arms is cheap and
slow, Kuchler is light and expensive, Kamenev is cheap and crude. **Same damage.** Add weight and
money to War World's brand axis and the brands stop being flavour text and start being a decision —
without adding a single point of damage to the game.

### 9.10 DO NOT steal

- **227 elements per item.** Roughly 90 of them are the five HSV-shifted graphics blocks and three
  sound blocks. Infantry needed that because it was compositing 2D sprite blobs; War World is
  Three.js and gets the same variety from materials.
- **16 per-terrain energy costs on every weapon** (§8.6) — an undocumented 16-wide array on 156 items
  is exactly the kind of field that goes unused and then can't be removed.
- **Two separate caps (`Max Allowed Soft` / `Hard`)** without a documented distinction. Pick one.
