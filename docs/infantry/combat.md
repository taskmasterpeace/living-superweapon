# INFANTRY ONLINE — HOW COMBAT ACTUALLY WORKED

Research file for **War World** (`D:\git\ShootEM`). Everything here is sourced. Every section is
marked **CONFIRMED** / **LIKELY** / **UNVERIFIED**.

## ⚠ Read this before trusting any number

**Infantry had no single balance.** Every *zone* (server) shipped its own item database, built by
players with the game's own editing tools. Giant Bomb: *"Game types and maps in Infantry are split
into separate servers called 'Zones'"* and *"Infantry's editing tools have produced a multitude of
different game types and maps, not only from developers themselves but also from ambitious players"*
([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Infantry)). Two zones could disagree about whether
energy even *was* a shield — and did (see §5).

So the numbers below come from three tiers of source, and I say which is which:

| Tier | Source | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **A — the engine itself** | [`InfantryOnline/Infantry-Online-Server`](https://github.com/InfantryOnline/Infantry-Online-Server) (C# emulator) + [`InfantryOnline/Zone-Assets`](https://github.com/InfantryOnline/Zone-Assets) (real shipped zone files) + [`iceHelp.cfg`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jcmcbeth/JoelMcBethWebsite/master/JoelMcBethWebsite/wwwroot/inf/files/iceHelp.cfg) (the CFG editor's own key documentation) | Ground truth for **field names, units and shipped values**. Real `.itm`/`.veh`/`.cfg` records decoded against the parser schema. ⚠ The emulator is a *reimplementation* — some resolution (notably the damage/shield math) lived in the closed-source client and is **not** in it. |
| **B — primary, developer prose** | [`freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iom*.htm`](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomindex.html) | The actual 1999 Harmless Games manual for **v0.25 — a closed beta**. States design intent. ⚠ Beta values are explicitly provisional (*"For this test everyones hit points will start at 30"*). |
| **B — primary, extracted** | `cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/` (the "42nd Mobile Infantry" squad site, c. 2000–01) | A fan-run **dump of the CTF zone's own item database**, self-described: *"This is a test page generated by a C++ program and an AWK script."* Field names match the engine's schema. Highest-fidelity *balance* numbers available. **ONE zone (CTF), ONE point in time.** |
| **C — descriptive** | Giant Bomb wiki, TV Tropes, freeinfantry.com, Steam reviews | Written by players. Good for behaviour and feel, not exact values. |

### Units (Tier A, CONFIRMED)

- **Time is centiseconds.** The server multiplies by 10 to get ms: `_fireDelay = _primaryProjectile.fireDelay * 10;` — `ZoneServer/Game/Arena/Bot/WeaponController.cs:120`
- **Distance is pixels; 1 map block = 16 px.** `TerrainLookup[_tiles[((y / 16) * _levelWidth) + (x / 16)]...]` — `ZoneServer/Game/Arena/Arena.cs:306`. **LOS ranges are in blocks**, item pickup in pixels.
- **Damage and energy are ×1000-scaled integers** (`EnergyDefaultMax=400000`, `kineticDamageInner=15000`).
- **Asset split:** `.cfg` = arena rules · **`.veh` = player bodies** (HP, energy, speed, LOS, armour) · **`.itm` = weapons/items**. Both `.itm` (v57) and `.veh` (v51) are positional CSV, one record per line.

## 1. THE CORE LOOP — **LIKELY** (mechanics CONFIRMED, tempo inferred)

**A minute of Infantry was a push.** Not a series of duels — an assault, with a shopping trip at
each end. The squad playbook is blunt about the rhythm:

> *"Death is just another way to get to the Dropship without wasting energy. Since you can only carry
> 2 energizers, don't plan on living beyond each 'play'."*
> — [42nd Mobile Infantry playbook](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/playbook.html)

That single line tells you more about the loop than any stat. **Death was cheap and expected, and
respawning was a resupply.** You spawned at your team's drop ship, which was *also the shop*:

> *"Each team starts in a drop ship which also serves as a shop and respawn point. The drop ship has
> a teleporter which spawns you ground-side."* — [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Infantry)

And money didn't persist: *"Money resets each login to $500,000... Do not be afraid to spend lots of
money fighting"* ([playbook](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/playbook.html)). A CTF loadout ran
$56k–$70k, so you could re-kit roughly seven times per session at no real cost.

### Time-to-kill — CONFIRMED, computed from the CTF zone's own tables

The armour tables give damage-per-nth-hit against a full shield with **zero time elapsed between
hits** — i.e. the theoretical floor. Against **Carapace + PF Generator (600 energy)**, an
Infantryman with **60 hit points**:

| Weapon | Hits before ANY health damage | Cumulative HP damage | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maklov AR mk 606 (standard AR) | **3** | 52 after 11 hits | ~12–13 hits to kill |
| SiG Arms m2 AS (auto shotgun) | **29** | 0 after 11 groups | Cannot kill this armour |
| Kuchler A6 CAW | **5** | 2 after 11 hits | Effectively cannot kill it |
| Recoiless Rifle | **0** | 18 / 41 / 66 per hit | **2 hits** |
| Particle Accelerator (sniper) | **0** | **60 on hit one** | **1 hit — instant kill through a full shield** |

Source: [armor analysis](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/armor.html).

The Maklov AR mk 606 fires **3 rounds at 100 ms, then reloads for 1200 ms** — an effective ~2.1
rounds/sec. Twelve connecting rounds is therefore **~5.5–6 seconds of unbroken hits, best case**,
before any energy regenerates and before a single miss. Against a like-armoured opponent who is
dodging, small-arms fights were long.

**So the game had two lethality regimes at once, by design:**
- **Small arms:** near-zero lethality against a charged shield. Their job is to *strip energy*.
- **Heavy weapons, explosives, snipers:** ignore the shield entirely (BREAK = 100%, see §5) and kill
  in 1–3 hits.

That split is the engine of the whole game. Rifles set up; heavies finish.

### Engagement ranges — CONFIRMED (range = muzzle velocity × lifetime)

Infantry has **no range falloff parameter**. A weapon's range is a *consequence* of how fast its
projectile flies and how long it lives. From the [weapons database](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/weapons.html):

| Weapon | Muzzle velocity | Lifetime | ⇒ Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incinerator (flamethrower) | 7.26 m/s | 0.80 s | **5.8 m** |
| Hand To Hand | 7.26 m/s | 0.60 s | 4.4 m |
| Frag Grenade (ballistic) | 7.26 m/s | 2.50 s | ~18 m |
| Steiner SMG 10a | 13.79 m/s | 1.50 s | **20.7 m** |
| Titan Arms mk III SG (shotgun) | 13.79 m/s | 1.50 s | 20.7 m |
| Maklov GL 8a (grenade launcher) | 7.26 m/s | 4.00 s | ~29 m |
| Titan Arms 9mm Carbine | 14.52 m/s | 3.00 s | 43.6 m |
| RPG | 17.78 m/s | 2.50 s | 44 m |
| Plasma Pistol / Kamenev PR K111r | 14.52 m/s | 6.00 s | 87 m |
| Maklov g9 Sniper | 25.40 m/s | 6.00 s | 152 m |
| Rocket Turret | 17.42 m/s | 10.00 s | 174 m |
| Particle Accelerator | 32.66 m/s | 10.00 s | **327 m** |

A **16× spread** between the flamethrower and the sniper, produced entirely by two numbers. Typical
infantry fighting happened inside ~20–45 m.

## 2. MOVEMENT — **CONFIRMED**

### It is not twin-stick. It is tank controls.

This surprises everyone. The default scheme from the [1999 manual](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomcontrol.htm):

> *"Rotate Left - Left Arrow key. Rotate Right - Right Arrow key. Both these keys will turn your man
> or vehicle in a clockwise or anti clockwise direction respectively."*
> *"Walk Forward - Up Arrow key. Walk Back - back arrow key."*

Movement is relative to **your character's own heading**, not the screen. A 25-year player says it
plainly, and identifies it as the source of the skill ceiling:

> *"This is isometric, but movement is based on character heading instead of map heading. This makes
> for a steeper learning curve, but a much higher skill cap than traditional twin-stick controls or
> MOBAs. The movement is also physics-based. You have friction & momentum, more with vehicles than on
> foot. Some weapons can bounce you around or suck you in, in ways that go beyond MOBAs."*
> — **brized**, [Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2830720/reviews/)

> *"the controls are Resident Evil tank style but worse"* — **Ignorance**, same source (recommended
> the game anyway)

> *"the movement is hard and at first really weird but is very rewarding when dodging and dueling"*
> — **Marqui2156**, same source

Competitive players moved to a hybrid: **keyboard strafes, mouse rotates.**

> *"One hand strafes, the other hand moves the mouse to rotate your characer."* — [42nd training page](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/training.html)

### Two dedicated defensive moves — CONFIRMED

Both are man-only, unavailable to vehicles ([manual](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomcontrol.htm)):

> *"Roll left - Alt+Left / Roll right - Alt + Right. Only available for the man and not for vehicles.
> **The man performs a side roll to dodge bullets/ missiles.**"*
> *"Crouch/Duck - Alt+Down. Available only to the man this **ducks the player behind a crate or other
> object.**"*

A discrete dodge and a discrete take-cover, both bound to a modifier + direction. The developers
listed this as a reason to fight on foot rather than in a vehicle: *"do you value the ability of the
man on foot to get into cover quickly and roll out of trouble?"*
([v25 guide](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomversion25.htm))

### Speed is per-class and it is a real spread — CONFIRMED

From the [characters page](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomcharacters.htm):

| Class | Energy | Max load | Max health | Top speed | Strafe/Thrust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heavy Weapons | 650 | 60 kg | 60 | **1200** | Strafe 60 |
| Infantryman | 600 | 50 kg | 60 | 1400 | Thrust 50 |
| Combat Engineer | 500 | 60 kg | 50 | 1600 | Thrust 50 |
| Field Medic | 500 | 50 kg | 50 | 1800 | Thrust 50 |
| Jump Trooper | 400 | 40 kg | 50 | 1900 | Thrust 60 |
| Commando (Infiltrator) | **800** | 30 kg | 50 | **2000** | Thrust 70 |

**A 1.67× top-speed spread across the roster**, and note the inversion: the fastest class has the
*smallest* carrying capacity and the *largest* energy pool.

### In the engine, speed is defined PER TERRAIN TYPE — CONFIRMED (Tier A)

This is the structural fact the manual's single "Top Speed" number hides. Each vehicle carries
**16 `SpeedValues` structs — one per terrain type** (`Assets/Shared/SpeedValues.cs`,
`VehInfo.Car.cs:7`), each with ten fields:

```
RollAllowed, RollFriction, RollThrust, RollRotate, RollTopSpeed,
StrafeThrust, HyperTopSpeed, HyperThrust, BackwardThrust, BackwardHyperThrust
```

**Every one of those is retuned per surface.** Mud, ice, road, interior floor and open ground can
each have their own friction, thrust, rotation rate, top speed, strafe strength and reverse speed.

Decoded from the shipped `bughunt.veh`, terrain 0:

| Class | RollFriction | RollThrust | RollRotate | RollTopSpeed | StrafeThrust | BackwardThrust | Hyper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marine | 40 | 65 | 1400 | 3000 | **65** | **63** | 0 (disabled) |
| Medic | 40 | 65 | 1400 | 3000 | 60 | 58 | 0 |
| Sergeant | 40 | 65 | 1400 | 3000 | 60 | 58 | 0 |

Note how that zone differentiates its classes: **identical top speed, identical rotation — the
Marine is separated purely by strafe and reverse thrust.** In a game where survival is dodging,
strafe authority *is* the combat stat.

Shared tail values: `BouncePercent=750` (75% of speed retained bouncing off a wall),
`ThrustEffectSpeed=4000`, `ThrustDelay=10`, `CatchLipPercent=1000`.

### Sprint exists — it is called "Hyper", and it costs ENERGY, not stamina — CONFIRMED

`HyperTopSpeed` / `HyperThrust` are the sprint speeds; the cost field is **`HyperEnergyCost`**
(`Assets/Veh/VehInfo.Car.cs:19`). **There is no stamina system anywhere in the schema.** Sprinting
draws on the same battery as your shield and your guns (§5). In BugHunt, foot classes have hyper
disabled entirely (`HyperTopSpeed = 0`).

### The other limiter is WEIGHT. — CONFIRMED

> *"ENCUMBERANCE. Put simply — the more you carry the slower you move. Each player can carry upto
> 15kg before it starts to affect his speed. Once you reach 30Kg then you will be unable to move."*
> — [v25 guide](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomversion25.htm)

(Those are 1999-beta caps; the live CTF zone ran 30–60 kg limits per class, and loadouts were
tuned to sit at exactly *"0% overweight"* — see the [loadouts page](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/loadouts.html),
where the Defensive Engineer knowingly runs *"10% overweight"*.)

Engine fields (Tier A): per-vehicle `NormalWeight` / `StopWeight` (`.veh` Elements 48/49) and
per-zone `[Soul] WeightNormalMax` / `WeightStopMax` — shipped as `25000` / `60000` in BugHunt and
BoomBallX, `40000` stop weight in GB_avp_hw. **Two thresholds, not a curve:** below `NormalWeight`
you are unaffected, above `StopWeight` you cannot move at all.

Encumbrance is also **per-vehicle**, which makes vehicle choice a build decision:

> *"The M1 jetpack can allow the player to carry 30Kg before it slows down. The Hoverboard starts to
> slow down when the player is carrying only 10kg. Thus the jetpack is great for people who want to
> be human tanks but the hoverboard is best suited for those who want to be a fast lightly armed
> skirmisher."*

Armour and gear also carry explicit `Speed Inc/Dec`, `Hyperspeed Inc/Dec`, `Rotation Inc/Dec` and
`Thrust Inc/Dec` percentage fields ([weapons DB](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/weapons.html)),
so any item could modify movement — though in the CTF armour set they were all 0.0%.

### Movement vs shooting — CONFIRMED NEGATIVE: moving does not hurt your aim, ever

**There is no move-accuracy field anywhere in the projectile schema.** A full grep of the `.itm`
schema and of `iceHelp.cfg` turns up nothing. Accuracy in Infantry is a **fixed random spread cone**
— `lowFireAngle` / `highFireAngle` and `lowRotationAngle` / `highRotationAngle`, plus `recoil` and
`verticle` — and those values do not change when you move. The only movement interaction in the
whole schema is **`movementCancelsPrefire`**: moving cancels a *charge-up*, not a shot.

That is a deliberate design position, not an omission. **Your bullets are equally accurate standing
still and sprinting**, so nothing ever punishes you for keeping moving — and in a game whose entire
survival model is "keep moving", a spread penalty would have fought the core loop.

Instead, Infantry uses an absolute **cannot-fire-while-moving** gate on its heaviest weapons:

> *"Heavy machine guns can put out way more shots than other guns but **can't be fired while the user
> is moving.** In Infantry dodging projectiles is essential to survival so using a heavy machine gun
> is a death sentence against any decent opponents."* — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/InfantryOnline)

> *"Also the firer **can not move when using this weapon.** This weapon uses a considerable amount of
> energy."* — Particle Accelerator description, [weapons DB](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/weapons.html)

That is a much sharper design than an accuracy cone: it converts "the biggest gun" into "the biggest
gun **and a stationary target**", and it is legible to everyone in the fight.

## 3. AIMING AND PROJECTILES — **CONFIRMED**

### Every projectile is simulated. Nothing is hitscan. Including the sniper.

> *"When you shoot, you do not instantaneously hit. **Even the sniper rifles don't hit immediately.**
> This means there is often a constant stream of projectiles flying all over the place."*
> — **Rob**, [Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2830720/reviews/)

> *"Almost every game mode has slow projectiles as this is a major component of skillful Infantry
> Online gameplay. **Shooters must exploit the game's physics and environments to make their shots
> hard to avoid while simultaneously using the same to avoid return fire themselves.**"*
> — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/InfantryOnline)

**Do the arithmetic on how slow this is.** An SMG round travels 13.79 m/s. At a 15 m engagement it
arrives **1.09 seconds** after you pull the trigger. At the SMG's maximum 20.7 m range it arrives in
**1.5 seconds**. You are not leading a target by a hair; you are leading it by a second and a half,
across a map where everyone is strafing unpredictably.

Aiming aids were built for exactly this. The 42nd's training page describes both:

> *"you can look at the arrow to tell how far you're leading, instead of having to constantly check
> the green dotted line."* — [training](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/training.html)

And the four skills they taught: *"hand-eye coordination, incoming vector assessment, leading shots,
and adaptability/variation."* Their aiming doctrine:

> *"Aiming is more important than dodging. All the dodging in the world won't kill anything unless
> you can shoot at someone and hit."*
> *"Never strafe regularly, always change the interval at which you change directions often"* and
> *"shift directions right before your opponent's weapon is about to fire."*
> — [playbook](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/playbook.html)

That last line is the game in miniature: the dodge is timed against the **enemy's fire delay**, not
against the bullet.

### The per-projectile physics fields — CONFIRMED at engine level

The player-facing dump ([weapons DB](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/weapons.html)) exposes:

```
Fire Delay / Reload Delay / Ammo Capacity / Ammo Per Shot / Energy Cost
Fire Angle / Friction (%/s) / Muzzle Velocity / Lifetime
Wall Bounces / Floor Bounces / Proximity Radius / Ballistic (Yes|No)
Damage: {Kinetic, Explosive, Plasma, Gas/Chemical, Armor Piercing, Energy Drain} × {Inner, Outer, Blast Radius}
```

The engine's actual `.itm` projectile schema (`Assets/Itm/ItmInfo.Projectile.cs`, 0-based CSV index):

```
muzzleVelocity[101]  gravityAcceleration[102]  horizontalFriction[93]  inheritedSpeed[94]
horizontalBounceSpeed[103]  bounceCount[104]
floorBounceVerticalSpeed[105]  floorBounceHorizontalSpeed[106]  floorBounceCount[107]
proximityRadius[108]  triggerWeight[109]  aliveTime[110]
fireDelay[54]  fireDelayOther[55]  maxFireDelay[56]  reloadDelayNormal[58]  reloadDelayPartial[59]
ammoCapacity[33]  ammoUsedPerShot[32]  prefireDelay[68]  reliability[69]
terrain0EnergyCost .. terrain15EnergyCost[36..51]   ← per-terrain firing cost
antiEffectsRadius[134] antiEffectsRecharge[135] antiEffectsFire[136]
antiEffectsThrust[137] antiEffectsRotate[138]      ← EMP, per-subsystem
6 damage channels × {Radius, Inner, Outer, Mode}[151..174]
```

**Real values decoded from shipped `bughunt.itm`:**

| Weapon | muzzleVel | gravity | wall bounces | floor bounces | proxRadius | fireDelay | reload | ammo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sniper Rifle | **15000** | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2000 ms | 3000 ms | 1 |
| Assault Rifle | **7000** | 0 | 0 | 0 | 8 | **220 ms** (~4.5/s) | 1500 ms | 30 |
| LAW (rocket) | **5000** | 0 | 0 | 0 | 10 (triggerWeight 50000) | 850 ms | 2500 ms | 1 |
| Hand Grenade | **2000** | **−22** | **5** | 1 (vert 750 / horz 650) | 0 | 200 ms | 1500 ms | 1 |
| Flamethrower | **2000** | 0 | 0 | (1000/1000) | 16 | 200 ms | 2000 ms | 8 |

Three things fall out of that table:

1. **A 7.5× muzzle-velocity spread** between the sniper and the grenade — travel time is a primary
   design axis, not a rounding detail.
2. **Gravity is opt-in per projectile.** Only the thrown grenade has it (`−22`; the zone default is
   `[Level] Gravity`, documented as `-25 = default`). Everything else flies flat.
3. **Wall bounces and floor bounces are separate counters with separate speed retention.** The
   grenade bounces off walls five times at 65% horizontal speed but off the floor only once, at 75%
   vertical / 65% horizontal. That asymmetry is what makes a grenade skitter down a corridor rather
   than bounce like a ball.

⚠ `aliveTime` came back **negative** on every weapon decoded (−100 to −400). The sign convention is
unknown — do not assume it maps directly to the player-facing "Lifetime" seconds above.

**EMP is per-subsystem.** `antiEffectsRecharge` / `antiEffectsFire` / `antiEffectsThrust` /
`antiEffectsRotate` are four independent suppressions on one projectile. A weapon can stop you
*recharging* without stopping you *shooting*, or freeze your rotation while leaving your thrust — a
much finer instrument than a generic stun.

- **`Ballistic: Yes`** = thrown/arcing (grenades, grenade launcher). `Fire Angle: 33 d` on the frag
  grenade is its launch arc. Bullets are `Ballistic: No`, `Fire Angle: 0 d`.
- **`Friction`** — projectiles can decelerate. (All 0.00%/s in the CTF set.)
- **`Proximity Radius`** — detonate near a target without touching it (Rocket Turret 0.73 m).
- **`Inner` vs `Outer` damage** — a direct hit vs a splash hit are separate numbers, per damage type.

### Cover blocks shots geometrically — and so do teammates — CONFIRMED

> *"**It's impossible to damage your teammates** in the vast majority of game modes. However **if your
> shot hits an ally it will be blocked by their body**, which somewhat limits the amount of Bullet
> Hell projectile spam possible in team games."* — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/InfantryOnline)

This is an underrated rule. No friendly fire, but **friendly bodies still occlude**. It makes
formation and lane discipline matter without ever punishing a player for a teammate's blunder — and
it is *why* stacking six people in a doorway does not produce six times the fire.

### Ricochet is a first-class, per-weapon integer — CONFIRMED

> *"Most Energy-type guns shoot projectiles that can ricochet off obstacles a certain number of
> times, giving them some **indirect fire capability**."* — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/InfantryOnline)

Observed values: Plasma Pistol `Wall Bounces: 2`, Kamenev PR K111r `1`, Frag Grenade `Wall Bounces: 3
/ Floor Bounces: 2`, Maklov GL 8a `Wall Bounces: 5 / Floor Bounces: 5`, all bullets `0`.

**Ballistic weapons bounce, kinetic weapons don't.** That is what makes energy weapons and grenade
launchers the *indoor* weapons — a bouncing projectile is a weapon that works around a corner.

Grenades additionally inherit your momentum:

> *"Hand grenades in Infantry Online bounce off terrain, explode on contact with an enemy, and
> **inherit most of the throwers movement momentum**. All these features make it possible for a
> skilled player to pull off impressively precise 'trick shot' grenade tosses."* — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/InfantryOnline)

### The zoom-out is the sniper scope — CONFIRMED

> *"Adjust view out - Shift + Up / Adjust view In - Shift + Down. Moves your vision out away from
> your player or back. **This is commonly refered to as the 'Sniper' mode.**"* — [manual](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomcontrol.htm)

TV Tropes is scathing about its practical value, and the critique is worth reading before you build
anything like it:

> *"aiming at off screen targets involves an awkward control scheme for 'zooming out' to view
> off-screen areas, **leaves the user unaware of what's near him while aiming this way**, and is
> generally more trouble than it's worth. Therefore long range guns will still mostly be used against
> targets directly in front of the shooter."*

## 4. HEALTH, ARMOUR AND DAMAGE — **CONFIRMED**

### Health is tiny, flat, and does not regenerate

> *"HITPOINTS is a represenation of how much damage your body has actually taken. For this test
> everyones hit points will start at 30."* — [v25 guide](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomversion25.htm)

Live CTF values were **50–60 HP** ([characters](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomcharacters.htm)).
Against a 400–800 energy pool, that means **energy is 8–16× the size of the health bar.** Almost all
combat happens in the shield layer; health is a small brittle thing underneath it.

> *"**Health can only be restored by a Medic or by using stim packs.**"* — [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Infantry)

No passive health regen. Ever. You carry **one** Stim Pack (`Max Allowed(Hard): 1`) or you find a
Medic.

### Six damage channels — CONFIRMED, and the count is fixed in the engine

The CTF zone showed `Kinetic · Explosive · Plasma · Gas/Chemical · Armor Piercing · Energy Drain`
([weapons DB](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/weapons.html)). BugHunt's `.veh` armour block
names them `Kinetic · Explosive · Electronic · Psionic · Bypass · Drain`.

**They are the same six slots, renamed.** The engine hard-codes **exactly six damage channels** —
every projectile has six `{Radius, Inner, Outer, Mode}` groups (`ItmInfo.Projectile.cs:151–174`),
every vehicle has six armour channels × four parameters (`.veh` Elements 10–33,
`Assets/Shared/ArmorValues.cs`) — and each zone supplies **display names** via
`[DamageType] Name0..Name5`. BugHunt ships `Name0=Kinetic, Name1=Explosive, Name2=Armor Piercing`.

That is a genuinely good piece of architecture: **six fixed channels, zone-authored labels.** A
sci-fi zone calls slot 3 "Psionic", a modern-military zone calls it "Incendiary", and the engine,
the armour tables and the balance maths never change.

⚠ The 1:1 mapping of channel index to display name is **INFERRED** — six schema channels, six name
slots, six armour channels is a clean correspondence, but no document states it explicitly. Likewise
**"Bypass" is almost certainly the shield-piercing channel and "Drain" the shields-only channel**,
matching the manual's *"Some weapons are capable of bypassing sheilds and other weapons only attack
the players sheilds"* — but that is my reading, not a cited fact.

Each armour channel carries four parameters, not one: **`Self Ignore`, `Pass Ignore`,
`Self Reduction`, `Pass Damage`** (`Docs/Veh/Shared.txt:10-33`) — i.e. flat-ignore and
percentage-reduction, computed separately for damage *to the vehicle* and damage *passed through to
the occupant*. That second axis is how a bunker protects the man inside it.

Note that **Energy Drain is a damage channel in its own right**, sitting on the same table as
kinetic — see §5.

> *"Most game modes contain at least two damage types: Energy and Kinetic. **Energy shots are usually
> better at draining the target's Energy while Kinetic typically drains more Hit Points.**"*
> — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/InfantryOnline)

### Armour is two numbers per damage type: a flat IGNORE and a percentage PROTECT

From the armour entries ([weapons DB](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/weapons.html)):

| Armour | Kinetic | Explosive | Energy Drain | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ceramax Combat Armor | 1.0 / 10% | 1.0 / 10% | 1.0 / 10% | 2 kg |
| Kevlite Combat Armor | 1.0 / 20% | 1.0 / 20% | 2.0 / 20% | 4 kg |
| CMP6 Assault Armor | 1.0 / 30% | 1.0 / 30% | 3.0 / 25% | 6 kg |
| **PF Generator** | 1.0 / 10% | — | **10.0 / 30%** | 2 kg, **−10 kJ/s** |
| **IDF Generator** | **4.0 / 30%** | 2.0 / 20% | — | 3 kg, **−10 kJ/s** |

The two generators are a genuine fork, not a power tier: **PF protects your shield, IDF protects your
body.** Their descriptions:

> PF: *"creates an energy barrier around the user that **absorbs energy weapons and dampens the suit
> energy loss effects** of weapons."*
> IDF: *"creates a barrier around the user that has startling effects upon projectiles. **It either
> slows them down to speeds where they do not cause damage, or stops them outright.** ... This unit
> uses a great deal of energy, so only use it sparingly."*

And they invert your matchups. Against the **SiG Arms m2 AS** auto-shotgun, Carapace + **PF** takes
**29 hits** before losing a hit point; Carapace + **IDF** takes **15**. Against the **Maklov AR 606**,
PF gives you 3 hits, IDF gives you 3 — but IDF drops the eventual per-hit damage curve much faster.
([armor analysis](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/armor.html))

Health itself is `.veh` **Element 7** (`VehInfo.cs:53 public int Hitpoints`). Shipped BugHunt values:
**Marine, Medic and Sergeant are all `HP=100`, `Weight=10000`** — that zone made every class equally
fragile and differentiated them on strafe thrust and kit instead.

### Healing and respawn — CONFIRMED, with the exact respawn formula

- **Medikit:** *"Heals all friendlies in an area over a period of time. Requires 1 Tsolvy Crystal per
  use."* Deluxe version costs 5 crystals. Area-of-effect, resource-consuming, Field-Medic-only. At
  engine level a medic's tool is **item type 11, `RepairItem`** (`ItmInfo.cs:62`); BugHunt ships
  `"MediKit"` as type 11.
- **Stim Pack:** *"an ampule of stimulant and HyperCortisone that allows the user to heal a small
  amount of damage. **These are created by Medics.**"* — a medic *manufactures* the consumable other
  players carry.
- **The ground can heal you.** Per-terrain `HealthRate=Health Recharge Rate` and `RepairRate=Vehicle
  Repair rate` in `[Terrain0..15]` (`iceHelp.cfg`) — a tile type can be a med bay, or a hazard.
- **Respawn delay = `[Timing] EnterDelay × 10 ms`** — CONFIRMED in the server source:
  ```csharp
  if (player._deathTime != 0 && now - player._deathTime > _server._zoneConfig.timing.enterDelay * 10)
  ```
  `ZoneServer/Game/Arena/Arena.cs:581`. Shipped: **BugHunt `EnterDelay=800` → 8.0 s**;
  **BoomBallX `EnterDelay=150` → 1.5 s.** A single zone-level dial, and the spread across shipped
  zones is **5.3×** — a co-op survival zone makes death cost eight seconds, a sports zone a second
  and a half.
- **Where:** the team drop ship, which is also the shop, plus *"a dynamic portal system [that] allows
  for fast warping to enemy/home"* ([freeinfantry.com](https://www.freeinfantry.com/)).
- **Spawn camping** was solved with **invincible turrets**: *"Invincible Sentry Guns also defend the
  spawn points in many game modes to discourage Spawn Camping."* ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/InfantryOnline))

## 5. ENERGY — **CONFIRMED, primary source**

This is the system. Here is the developers' own 1999 description, in full:

> **"ENERGY & HIT POINTS**
> Each player starts off with his own **battery of energy and a pre set charge rate**. Energy is used
> to fire some weapons and also acts as a sheild in the game. Your current energy level is located in
> the upper left of the screen... **When you fire a weapon such as a laser pistol your energy will go
> down a small amount. Constant firing of energy weapons will reduce your energy to zero.**
>
> Energy also acts as a sheild. **The amount of energy you have in relation to the maximum energy is
> expressed as a %. For example if your maximum is 500 energy then if your energy goes down to 250
> then your sheilds will be at 50%. This % is the amount of damage that is stopped from being passed
> on to your characters HIT POINTS.**
>
> ...**Some weapons are capable of bypassing sheilds and other weapons only attack the players
> sheilds.**"
> — [Infantry manual, v0.25, Harmless Games](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomversion25.htm)

### ⚠ But the exact shield formula is NOT fully resolved — read this before copying it

Two authoritative sources describe **two different models**:

- **The manual (v0.25 beta) says PROPORTIONAL MITIGATION.** At 50% energy you block 50% of incoming
  damage; energy is a *percentage multiplier* on damage reduction.
- **`iceHelp.cfg`, the CFG editor's own key documentation, says DEPLETING POOL:**
  ```
  [Soul]
  EnergyShieldMode=Energy shields are enabled. Energy takes damage before affecting health. (1=Yes, 0=No)
  ```
  i.e. energy is a buffer consumed *before* HP — closer to a conventional overshield.

**The server emulator parses `energyShieldMode` (`CfgInfo.Soul.cs:32`) and then never reads it.** The
damage/shield resolution lived in the **closed-source client**, so the emulator cannot settle it and
neither can I. And ⚠ **shipped zones set `EnergyShieldMode=2`, a value `iceHelp` does not document at
all** (it claims 1/0 only) — so there is at least one behaviour in production that nobody wrote down.

Shipped values (decoded from real zone `.cfg` files):

| Zone | `EnergyDefaultMax` | `EnergyDefaultRate` | `EnergyShieldMode` |
|---|---|---|---|
| BugHunt | 400000 | 100 | **0** (off) |
| BoomBallX | 400000 | 100 | **2** |
| GB_avp_hw | 600000 | 250 | **2** |

Note **the shield can be switched off entirely per zone** (BugHunt, a co-op PvE zone, runs mode 0) —
confirming at engine level what Giant Bomb described for Skirmish (§5, end).

The CTF zone's armour tables are the strongest evidence for the *shape* of the shipped behaviour,
and they are consistent with proportional mitigation plus a per-weapon threshold (see BREAK, below).
**I would build the manual's proportional model.** But the honest statement is: the exact curve is
unrecovered.

### The rule in one line

**Your shield strength is the fraction of your battery that is currently full — and the same battery
powers your weapons, your armour, your cloak and your sensors.**

Every offensive act makes you more killable. That is not a side effect; it is the entire design.
The developers put the lesson in the manual's tips section:

> *"Players should not be fooled into thinking that just becuase a weapon uses only energy it is
> better than one that uses ammo. **Remember when you fire an energy weapon you are effectivley
> making yourself weaker.** Quite often a good old fashioned reveolver can be more useful than a laser
> rifle."*

### What drains energy — CONFIRMED, four separate channels

**1. Firing certain weapons.** A per-shot `Energy Cost` field, in kJ:

| Weapon | Energy cost/shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Needler | 15 kJ | |
| Plasma Pistol | 20 kJ | *"Plasma weapons use suit energy, so they do not require ammunition."* |
| Kamenev PR K111r (plasma rifle) | 40 kJ | |
| Repulsor Field | 50 kJ | |
| **Particle Accelerator** | **400 kJ** | Half a Commando's entire 800 pool, **per shot**, `Ammo Capacity: 2` |
| Maklov AR 606, SMGs, shotguns, RPG, MG | **0 kJ** | Conventional weapons cost ammo instead |

**The Commando is the sharpest expression of the rule.** Fastest class, largest energy pool (800),
weakest armour — and *"All of the Infiltrator's weapons take energy to use, which make them an even
more vulnerable target in small skirmishes."* ([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Infantry)).
Fire the Particle Accelerator twice and you are at zero energy, standing still, with 50 HP and no
shield. That trade is the class.

⚠ At engine level the firing cost is **per terrain type**: `terrain0EnergyCost` … `terrain15EnergyCost`
(`ItmInfo.Projectile.cs:36-51`), plus `secondShotEnergy` / `secondShotTimeout` for a distinct
follow-up cost. **The same weapon can cost different energy to fire depending on what you are
standing on** — a design lever I have not seen anywhere else and did not find used in any zone I
could inspect.

**2. Running equipment — a continuous per-second drain.** This is the part everyone misses:

| Item | Rate |
|---|---|
| Cloaking Unit | **−18.00 kJ/sec** |
| PF Generator | **−10.00 kJ/sec** |
| IDF Generator | **−10.00 kJ/sec** |
| Sentry TD | −10.00 kJ/sec |
| Enhanced Sensors | −6.00 kJ/sec |
| Teleport Disruptor | −6.00 kJ/sec |
| **Suit SuperCharger** | **+10.00 kJ/sec** (*"Does not increase overall capacity, but does improve recharge rate"*) |
| Heavy Powercell | +250 kJ **capacity** (10 kg) |

Source: [weapons DB](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/weapons.html). The engine fields behind
this are **`EnergyCostPerTick`** (`.veh` Element 47 — a vehicle costs energy simply to *be* in) and
**`HyperEnergyCost`** (sprinting, §2). Utility items grant `Recharge Rate` (`Docs/Itm/Utility.txt:35`).

**Your shield generator burns your shield.** The thing that makes you tough is itself consuming the
resource that makes you tough. Running a PF Generator, Enhanced Sensors and a Teleport Disruptor at
once costs 22 kJ/s of standing-still upkeep. Cloaking alone costs 18 kJ/s. Nothing is free to have
switched on.

**3. Being shot.** `Energy Drain` is a full damage type on every weapon, alongside kinetic. Look at
the ratio:

| Weapon | Kinetic (inner) | **Energy Drain (inner)** |
|---|---|---|
| Steiner SMG 10a | 10 | **80** |
| Titan Arms 9mm Carbine | 10 | **120** |
| Steiner MG94k | 25 | **140** |
| Frag Grenade | 0 explosive 60 | **200** |
| Maklov g9 Sniper | 45 | **350** |
| Particle Accelerator | 80 (+40 AP) | **600** |
| Titan Arms mk III SG (per pellet, ×9) | 4 | **40** (×9 = 360 on a full connect) |

**Most of what a bullet does is strip your shield, not hurt you.** A shotgun blast that lands all
nine pellets removes 360 of a 600 pool and deals 36 kinetic. It does not kill you. It makes you
killable by the next thing.

**4. Being repulsed.** Dedicated *energy-attack* weapons that do no damage at all:

- **Repulsor Charge** (grenade): 200 energy drain, **9.29 m blast radius**, 0 damage of any kind.
- **Enhanced RFG** (Commando): **400 energy drain, 9.29 m radius**, 0 damage.
- **Repulsor Mine**: *"When triggered it releases a repulsor field, pushing everything in it's blast
  radius. **It also will drain suit energy, making for devious traps.**"*
- **EMP Grenade**, **EMP Mine**, **Haywire Grenade** — same family.

The 42nd's numbers: *"The Enhanced Repulsor Field Generator (ERFG) drains up to 400 energy in a
9.89 m radius"* and *"the ERFG draining up to 400 and the charge draining up to 200"*
([playbook](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/playbook.html)).

### What refills it

A **per-character `pre set charge rate`** ([manual](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomversion25.htm)),
modifiable by the Suit SuperCharger (+10 kJ/s). At engine level the zone default is
`[Soul] EnergyDefaultRate` (shipped: 100, or 250 in GB_avp_hw), overridden per class by `.veh`
Elements 95/96/97 (`Energy Max` / `Energy Rate` / `Energy Start`) — and `iceHelp` is explicit that
the zone value is *"Only used for default character. You should edit this in the .VEH file for
characters."*

**And the ground recharges you.** `[TerrainN] EnergyRate=Energy recharge rate` (`iceHelp.cfg`) — a
tile type can be a recharge pad, or drain you. Combined with per-terrain firing costs and per-terrain
movement (§2), **terrain in Infantry is a full combat parameter, not decoration.** A room can be
authored to be slow, expensive to shoot in, and to drain your shield.

Plus two consumables:

> *"The Energizer gives a quick jolt of energy to the user suit. The Flux Capacitor is drained upon
> use."* — `Max Allowed(Hard): 2`, [weapons DB](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/weapons.html)

> *"**Energizer has a reload time of 1.5 seconds, and gives 200 energy back.**"* — [playbook](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/playbook.html)

**You carry exactly two.** 400 energy of emergency reserve per life, on a 1.5 s animation. That is
the hard cap that makes the resource genuinely scarce, and it is why the playbook says *"don't plan
on living beyond each 'play'."* Combat Engineers could deploy an **Energizer Kit** for the team.

### What happens at zero — the death spiral is EXPLICIT (but zone-level, not engine-level)

⚠ **There is no zero-energy field or handler anywhere in the engine schema** — no prose in
`iceHelp.cfg`, no branch in the emulator. Everything below is behaviour observed and documented **in
the CTF zone**, and it may be client-side or emergent rather than a named system. It is nonetheless
consistently attested by three independent sources:

**(a) Your shield reaches 0% and every hit lands in full.** Directly from the manual's percentage
rule.

**(b) Your field generator switches off — so you lose your armour bonus too.** From the armour table
legend:

> *"This **DOES take into account the loss of the field generator due to hitting zero energy.**"*
> — [armor analysis](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/armor.html)

**Running out of energy does not just remove your shield. It removes your armour.** You fall two
layers at once. That is why the damage-per-hit curves in those tables ramp so steeply near the end.

**(c) Every powered device you were running shuts down.** The playbook weaponises this:

> *"ERFG or Repulsor Charge grenades drain defender energy. **If this reduces him to zero energy, his
> Teleport Disruptor will turn off.**"* — [playbook](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/playbook.html)

You do not have to kill a defender to break a defence. You have to **brown him out.** A single
9.29 m radius Repulsor Charge, doing zero damage, can switch off the device stopping your entire team
from teleporting in.

### The threshold refinement: BREAK

The armour tables reveal that the "proportional shield" rule resolves, per weapon-vs-armour pair,
into a **threshold**:

> *"**BREAK:** The exact level of energy that the wearer will take damage if inner hit by that
> category of weapon... **A value of zero means the wearer cannot be hurt by that type of weapon. A
> value equal to the max energy total means an inner hit will always damage the wearer, regardless of
> energy total.**"* — [armor analysis](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/armor.html)

So each pairing gets a readable "how many hits can I eat" number. Against Carapace + PF (600):

- **SiG Arms m2 AS** → `BREAK 0`, **29 hits**. This weapon *literally cannot kill you*.
- **Steiner SMG 10a** → `BREAK 66` (11%), **11 hits**.
- **Maklov AR 606** → `BREAK 385` (64%), **3 hits**.
- **RPG / grenades / mines / sniper / Particle Accelerator** → `BREAK 600` (100%), **0 hits — always
  damages regardless of shield**.

**That is the whole weapon triangle in one column.** Small arms are shield-strippers that cannot
finish. Heavies are finishers that ignore shields. The tactical loop — *strip, then finish* — is not
a convention players invented; it is forced by the numbers.

### ⚠ Two zones deliberately disagreed about energy, and the reason matters

**Skirmish** turned the shield off:

> *"In Skirmish... **energy is only used by support classes such as Medics and Squad Leaders to power
> their abilities and doesn't reduce health damage taken.** The result is that battles in Skirmish are
> longer-range, and a one-on-one duel will end much sooner than it might in CTF."*
> — [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Infantry)

**GravBall** deleted health and made energy the *only* pool:

> *"There is **no health** in GravBall. **Every bike has 600 energy, and once it reaches 0 the player
> dies.** In addition, the standard weapons and abilities costs energy to fire. **Accuracy and judging
> your opponent's energy level is the key to life or death.**"* — [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Infantry)

Same resource, three settings: shield + weapon fuel (CTF), weapon fuel only (Skirmish), the entire
life bar (GravBall). It scaled cleanly across all three.

## 6. TEAM PLAY AND ROLES — **CONFIRMED**

Seven CTF classes ([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Infantry), quoted directly):

- **Infantry** — *"Your basic ground soldier... have the best damage reduction capabilities from
  armor. Infantry are the key to any good offense or defense."*
- **Medic** — *"invaluable part of base defense. They have area of effect healing abilities... can
  use tranquilizer darts (and its energy-based counterpart, the 'stunner') **to freeze enemies in
  place for a few seconds.** The medic also has the ability to **summon other players to his
  position**, and can **prevent the enemy's use of teleportation or summoning abilities in a small
  radius.**"*
- **Engineer** — *"construct automatic turrets, which are the backbone of any well-defended base.
  There are four types... The fast-firing **MG turret**... The **Rocket turret** shoots much slower,
  but deals massive damage in a small radius and can even one-shot lesser armored targets... The
  **Plasma turret** fires a multitude of **energy-draining projectiles that bounce off walls.** The
  last is the **Sentry**, which doesn't have any offensive capability but instead warns you of
  incoming enemies and prevents use of teleportation and summoning abilities."*
- **Heavy Weapons** — *"who you call when you need to do a lot of splash damage or kill enemy turrets.
  Moving slower and having less armor than the Infantry, the heavy isn't suited to one-on-one
  skirmishes, but with a proper backing force can tear through the enemy's defenses with ease."*
- **Jump Trooper** — *"the most agile of the seven classes... **specialized armor that reduces damage
  done to their energy.** ...While on a **Deathboard**, the Jump Trooper can't strafe, but has
  incredible speed and **a deadly melee attack that bypasses shields.**"*
- **Infiltrator** — *"CTF's stealth class... can only be seen at range by turrets, or players with
  Enhanced Sensors... **Blink Generator: Using it once sets a beacon at that point, then using it a
  second time instantly teleports the Infiltrator back to that spot.**"*
- **Squad Leader** — *"second-best armored class... useful for **staking out a position near (or
  inside) the enemy's base and summoning in a constant flow of reinforcements.**"*

### The three abilities that make it a team game rather than seven solo players

1. **SUMMON.** Medic and Squad Leader can teleport teammates to their position. The Squad Leader's
   version *"uses less energy"* and is *"much faster"* ([weapons DB](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/weapons.html);
   [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Infantry)). One survivor inside the enemy base is a
   **spawn point**.
2. **DENIAL OF SUMMON.** The **Teleport Disruptor** *"will make all enemies in the area of effect
   unable to teleport"* — carried by Medics and Squad Leaders (−6 kJ/s), and mountable in a
   deployable **Sentry TD** (−10 kJ/s). Every base defence is fundamentally a summon-denial bubble.
3. **BROWNING OUT THE DENIAL.** Repulsors drain the defender to zero and the Disruptor dies (§5).

That is a genuinely three-layer objective system, and every layer runs on the same energy resource.

### Formal command — CONFIRMED

Chat channels: *"Main (arena-wide), Team (// or '), Chat (;), Squad (#), Enemy (\")"*, plus **sound
macros** — *"%22 = Acknowledged!, %23 = Affirmative!, %24 = Attacking Enemy!"*, with a discipline note:
*"Only select macros include sounds to avoid audio spam"* ([playbook](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/playbook.html)).
Private messages and squad chat worked **across every zone on the network**
([manual](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomcomms.htm); [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Infantry)).

**Players could not issue binding orders**, but there was a designated **Offensive Squad Leader** who
*"Makes play calls"*, and the team ran a numbered playbook — Siege, Snatch & Grab, Sneak Attack,
Backstab — with a per-role assignment sheet, e.g.:

> *"**Offensive Heavy Weapons (Anti-Turret):** SOLELY responsible for destroying turrets to allow the
> flaggers access to flags."*
> *"**Defensive Medic:** Base defense; auto-summons offensive players if overwhelmed; summons only for
> flags or emergency defense."*
> — [playbook](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/playbook.html)

**The command layer was social, not mechanical**, and it worked — see §9.

## 7. VEHICLES IN COMBAT — **LIKELY** (existence and stats CONFIRMED, tactical impact partly inferred)

- **Multi-crew is real and granular.** *"the player can select from six crew positions he wishes to
  command... Driver — he steers it. SMG turrets — the battle tank has four Submachine gun turrets in
  the Foward right to rear left postions. Turret — This will allow the player to use the main gun."*
  Plus: *"**the first player to enter a vehicle then establishes ownership of that vehicle and from

  — [manual](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/iomcontrol.htm)
- **Vehicles are HP objects, not damage-reduction states.** *"IF you enter a vehicle then you might
  see anther number below it such as **BUNKER 200**. This means the bunker position you are in has

- **Personal vehicles are loadout items** with their own encumbrance profile (§2): JumpPack,
  Hoverboard, Drop Pack, Deathboard, FlightPack. The Hoverboard *"Requires 2 light PowerCells per

- **Running people over is a weapon with a stat block.** `TANK : Squish` — *"Aim for the enemy under
  your vehicle."* 20 kinetic / **80 armor-piercing** / 200 energy drain, 2.32 m radius, 1500 ms
  cycle ([weapons DB](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/weapons.html)). Armor-piercing is the

- **Infantry counters:** RPG (Heavy Weapons only, 12 kg), Recoiless Rifle, Micro Missile Launcher,

- **Jetpacks trade agility for inertia:** *"The Jet packs greatly increase the user's top speed but
  also **increase his inertia and momentum, making it harder to change directions**"* ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/InfantryOnline)) —
  in a game where dodging is survival, speed is not straightforwardly an upgrade.

The 1999 feature list promised *"Tanks, buggies, jetpacks, hover boards, bikes, skimmers, flyers,
transport craft, ambulances and even tunneling machines"*
([features](https://freeinfantry.com/history/infantry/features.html)) — I have not verified which of
those actually shipped in the live zones.

## 8. INDOOR / ENCLOSED COMBAT — **CONFIRMED** (tactics and the LOS system; only camera *framing* unverified)

### The single most important finding in this file

Infantry did not have one combat model tuned for one map style. **It retuned projectile speed to
match how enclosed the map was**, and shipped both as separate zones:

> *"The biggest difference between Skirmish and CTF is **the speed of weapon projectiles: In CTF,
> projectiles move much more slowly than most action games which supports the more close-quarters
> nature of CTF maps. In Skirmish, the maps are much more open and projectiles move much faster.**...
> The result is that battles in Skirmish are longer-range, and a one-on-one duel will end much sooner
> than it might in CTF."* — [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Infantry)

**Slow projectiles + tight maps ⇒ dodging is possible in a corridor and fights last.**
**Fast projectiles + open maps ⇒ duels resolve quickly at range.**

And the *energy shield itself* was only switched on in the close-quarters mode. CTF has shields;
Skirmish does not. The close-quarters zone is the one that needed the long, layered, strip-then-finish
health model, because that is what makes a doorway fight into a fight rather than a coinflip.

### Doors are player-operated and they block fire — CONFIRMED

> *"**The cargo door must be opened and closed at select times to let rocket and machine gun fire to
> hit attackers.**"* — [42nd strategic maps](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/map.html)

A defender physically cycles a door between "my turrets have line of fire" and "I am sealed". That is
a two-state cover object with a real cost on both sides, operated by a human under pressure.

### Enclosure is a liability for the DEFENDER, not the attacker — CONFIRMED, and counter-intuitive

> *"**The only feasibly defendable bases against JT rushes are A7 and D7. All others are too enclosed
> to defend against grenade launcher spam.**"* — [42nd strategic maps](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/map.html)

> *"**Do not try to hold corners or doorways, instead stick to wide open areas** where Heavy Weapons
> with RPG's and LMG's can easily avoid the Grenade Launchers."*
> *"**Rush the enemy Jump Troopers do not fall back.**"* — [playbook](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/playbook.html)

Because grenades bounce (`Wall Bounces: 5 / Floor Bounces: 5` on the Maklov GL 8a) and explosives
ignore shields, a tight room is a **kill box for whoever is throwing in**. The defender's instinct —
hold the doorway — is explicitly wrong. Enclosure favours the side with area weapons, and that is
almost always the attacker.

### The breach procedure — CONFIRMED, and it is a real drill

The 42nd's grenade-rush sequence, verbatim:

> 1. *"Macro squad of incoming rush"*
> 2. *"Approach doorway, repulse bouncing fire"*
> 3. *"**Repulse through doorway into open area**"*
> 4. *"Lob grenades at clusters; charge forward"*
> 5. *"Use incinerators, shotguns, grenade launcher for cleanup"*

with the doorway rule stated separately:

> *"**Repulse while going through the doorway, use this time to get into an open area where you can
> dodge.**"* — [playbook](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~jhsu/infantry/playbook.html)

Read that carefully, because it is a complete design statement about enclosed combat:

**A doorway is the one place you cannot dodge.** Infantry's answer is not a smoke grenade or a
shield ability — it is the **Repulsor**, which sweeps *projectiles already in the air* out of the
threshold (50 kJ, 250 ms, 1500 ms cooldown). You spend energy to buy the half-second the geometry
denies you, and your goal on the far side is *space to dodge in again*. Mobility is the defence;
the doorway removes it; the repulsor rents it back at a price.

Note also the weapon selection: **incinerators (5.8 m range), shotguns (20.7 m), grenade launchers**.
Nothing at range. The 20 m weapons are the room-clearing weapons.

### The rest of the enclosed-combat picture — CONFIRMED

### How interiors actually worked: LOS TYPES — **CONFIRMED (Tier A)**

This is the mechanism, and it is better than anything I expected to find.

**Vision is a cone with a hard block-based range**, set per zone (`CfgInfo.Los.cs`), documented in
`iceHelp.cfg`:

```
DefaultDistance = Line of Sight distance by blocks. (The blocks you see when editing a map for vision/physics)
DefaultAngle    = Formula should be this: DefaultAngle*2 = Vision in degrees. 180 should be all around vision.
```

Shipped values:

| Zone | `DefaultDistance` | `DefaultAngle` | → field of view | `Xray` | `TeamVisionDistance` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BugHunt | 76 blocks (1216 px) | 100 | **200°** | 0 | 3096 |
| GB_avp_hw | 250 blocks | 180 | **360° all-round** | 0 | 1200 |
| BoomBallX | 2048 | 360 | all-round | **1** | 5000 |

So a survival-horror zone gives you a 200° cone, a sports zone gives you the whole map. Vehicles
override it: BugHunt's Marine ships `LOS Distance=100, LOS Angle=90` — a **180° cone, narrower than
its own zone default** (`.veh` Elements 133/134/135). **Sight is a per-class stat.**

**And now the part that answers the interior question.** The engine defines **eight LOS TYPES, each
carrying an 8×8 visibility matrix** (`CfgInfo.LosType.cs`):

```
[LosType0..7]
  SeeType0 .. SeeType7   ← can someone standing in type X see into type N?
  VisibleDistance        ← sight radius clamp while in this type
  Solid                  ← does it block entirely
```

Shipped `GB_avp_hw` — a graduated ladder of interior sight radii:

| LosType | `VisibleDistance` | `Solid` |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **4 blocks** (64 px) | 0 |
| 5 | **8 blocks** | 0 |
| 6 | **12 blocks** | 0 |
| 7 | **16 blocks** | 0 |

BugHunt: `LosType0` = `VisibleDistance=-1, Solid=0` (open ground, unlimited); `LosType1` = `-1,
Solid=1`; `LosType2` = `32, Solid=1`.

**A level designer paints sight range onto the floor.** Tag a corridor `LosType4` and everyone
standing in it is clamped to four blocks — sixty-four pixels — *regardless of the zone's 250-block
default*. Tag the room it opens into `LosType6` and sight opens back up to twelve. And the 8×8
`SeeTypeN` matrix means visibility is **directional and asymmetric between spaces**: you can author
a type that can see *out* into the open but not *in* from it, which is an ambush-from-a-doorway
primitive expressed purely as level data.

That is how Infantry did enclosed combat. Not occlusion geometry, not a roof-removal camera — **a
per-tile sight-radius field plus a room-to-room visibility matrix.** In a fixed-scale 2D isometric
game there is no third dimension to occlude and nothing to hide behind visually, so concealment was
made a *property of the floor you are standing on*.

⚠ **INFERRED:** that low-`VisibleDistance` LOS types are specifically how buildings and rooms clamp
sight is my reading of the shipped values — the mechanism is confirmed in source, the level-design
intent is not documented. `DefaultXray` and `TeamSharing` are parsed by the server but appear in no
documentation at all.

⚠ **Still UNVERIFIED: how the camera framed interiors** — whether walls were drawn over you, cut
away, or simply had no roof. No source. The manual points at a line-of-sight section (`See LOS
section`) that is **missing from the surviving archive** (`los.htm` and `iomlos.htm` both 404).

**Radar is a separate channel** and per-vehicle: `Display On Friendly Radar` / `Display On Enemy
Radar` plus colours (`.veh` Elements 139–142), with `[Flag] CarrierOnRadar` and
`CarrierLineOfSight` making the objective-carrier's visibility its own tunable.

# WHAT I COULD NOT FIND

1. **THE SHIPPED ENERGY-SHIELD FORMULA — the most important gap in this file.** The manual describes
   proportional mitigation; `iceHelp.cfg` describes a depleting pool consumed before HP. The server
   emulator **parses `energyShieldMode` and never uses it** — the maths lived in the closed-source
   client. Worse, production zones ship **`EnergyShieldMode=2`, a value `iceHelp` does not document**.
   The CTF armour tables are the best surviving evidence of the *shape*, and they favour the manual.
   I would build proportional. But it is not settled.
2. **What happens at zero energy — at engine level.** No field, no handler, no prose anywhere in the
   schema. The three consequences in §5 are attested for the CTF zone only and may be client-side or
   emergent.
3. **Whether energy regenerated while taking fire**, or was suppressed on hit. This decides whether
   sustained small-arms pressure can ever break a shield at all, and I found nothing on it.
4. **The unit for the manual's `Top Speed`** (1200–2000 per class). The engine works in pixels and
   centiseconds and stores `RollTopSpeed=3000` for BugHunt foot classes, but I could not tie the
   manual's class figures to the engine's units, so **I still cannot state the ratio of player speed
   to bullet speed** — the single most important tuning number in a slow-projectile game.
5. **`aliveTime` sign convention.** Negative on every weapon decoded (−100 to −400). Unknown whether
   this is a flag, a different unit, or a sentinel.
6. **How the camera framed interiors.** The *vision* mechanism is now confirmed (LOS types, §8) but
   how the renderer presented a player inside a building is still unsourced.
7. **`DefaultXray` and `TeamSharing`** are parsed by the server and documented nowhere.
8. **Boarding Action's layouts and rules** beyond one TV Tropes sentence about magazines — frustrating,
   because it is the zone most relevant to enclosed combat.
9. **The Maklov AR mk 606's muzzle velocity** — the source page truncated at that field. (100 ms fire
   delay, 3-round capacity, 1200 ms reload are confirmed.)
10. **Reddit testimony.** `reddit.com` blocks the crawler outright; every Reddit thread on the game
    was inaccessible. Steam and forum testimony had to carry §9.
11. **The freeinfantry.com forums** (`/forum/viewforum.php?f=26`, the CTF board) return HTTP 500. The
    deepest tactical discussion almost certainly lives there.
12. **No prose documentation exists for `.itm`/`.veh` fields at all** — only ordinal
    `Element N : Name : Type` lists in `Docs/`. There is no `iceHelp` equivalent for weapons or
    vehicles, and the two vehicle docs contradict each other (`Docs/Veh/Shared.txt` calls Elements
    110–125 "Soccer Prox Terrain 0..15"; `Docs/Veh/Car.txt` calls them bare "Terrain N"). The
    community's own `Car.txt` is **off by two** on where the terrain block starts.
13. **Per-zone variation.** Balance numbers here are from the **CTF zone, c. 2000–01**; engine values
    are from **BugHunt / BoomBallX / GB_avp_hw**. Every zone shipped its own database, and they

# WHAT WAR WORLD SHOULD STEAL

Ranked by how much they change the feel per hour of work.

### 1. Tune projectile speed to map enclosure — and say so in the design doc

Infantry shipped **two speeds of the same game**: CTF (slow projectiles, tight maps, shields on) and
Skirmish (fast projectiles, open maps, no shields). It did not compromise between them.

If enclosed combat is what War World does best, **commit to the CTF end and make it explicit**: slow
enough that a competent player can *see the shot coming and step out of it inside a room*. At 13.79
m/s a bullet crosses a 15 m room in 1.09 s. That is the number that makes indoor fighting a skill
expression rather than a coinflip, and it is the reason Infantry's tightest maps were also its most
loved. If you later want an open map, **change the projectile speed for that map**, don't rebalance
the guns.

### 2. Energy: one battery for shield, weapons, and every device you have switched on

Not a shield that recharges. **A single pool that your defence, your offence, and your gadgets all
draw from.** The developers' own sentence is the pitch:

> *"Remember when you fire an energy weapon you are effectivley making yourself weaker."*

Concretely, port all four drain channels — they are what make it a *system* rather than a bar:
- per-shot cost on *some* weapons only (so conventional guns stay a real choice)
- **continuous per-second upkeep on anything switched on** (this is the one everyone forgets; a
  shield generator that costs 10 kJ/s to run is a far better object than one that is free)
- energy damage as its own damage type on ordinary bullets, sized ~8–12× the health damage
- **pure drain weapons that do zero damage** (the Repulsor Charge: 200 drain, 9.29 m, 0 damage)

Then make **zero energy remove your armour too, not just your shield**. That is Infantry's cruellest
and best detail. Two layers fall at once, so the last 20% of a fight accelerates hard, and everyone
can feel the moment it tips.

### 3. Two hit-count numbers, surfaced in the UI: "shots I can eat" and "shots to kill"

Infantry's `BREAK` / `Hits` columns are, functionally, a readable statement of *"this weapon needs 3
hits before it can hurt you; that one hurts you immediately."* Three hits vs twenty-nine vs zero is a
spread a player can hold in their head.

Derive it and **show it**. It is the fix for **Ignorance**'s review — *"There are energy weapons too
which honestly I've never really understood"* — from a player who liked the game and still never
learned its signature mechanic.

### 4. Make small arms unable to finish, and heavies unable to miss

The strip-then-finish loop is not a convention Infantry's players invented; it falls out of the
numbers. Auto-shotgun: **29 hits and it still cannot kill you.** RPG: **BREAK 600 — always damages,
regardless of shield.**

This is what makes a squad more than a stack of individuals: the rifles genuinely cannot win alone,
and the heavy genuinely cannot survive alone (*"Moving slower and having less armor than the
Infantry, the heavy isn't suited to one-on-one skirmishes, but with a proper backing force can tear
through the enemy's defenses with ease"*).

### 5. The doorway problem, and the Repulsor as its answer

This is the highest-value item for a game about enclosed spaces, and it is a *complete* three-part
design:

1. **A doorway is the one place you cannot dodge.** Own that. Don't design it away.
2. **Give the attacker a purchasable half-second**, not immunity. The Repulsor sweeps projectiles
   *already in flight* out of the threshold. 50 kJ, 250 ms to fire, 1500 ms cooldown — so it is a
   timing decision, and spending it means you are less shielded on the far side.
3. **Make the objective on the far side "space to dodge in", not "kill the defender".** Verbatim
   doctrine: *"Repulse while going through the doorway, use this time to get into an open area where
   you can dodge."*

And steal the counter-intuitive consequence: **enclosure punishes the defender.** *"Do not try to hold
corners or doorways, instead stick to wide open areas."* Because grenades bounce off five walls and
explosives ignore shields, the tight room belongs to whoever is throwing *in*. If War World's rooms
currently favour the holder, this is the lever: give attackers **bouncing, shield-ignoring area
weapons with short range**, and holding a corner stops being free.

### 6. LOS TYPES — paint sight radius onto the floor. This is the enclosed-combat system.

If you take one structural idea from Infantry into War World, take this one. §8 has the detail; the
shape is:

- Eight **LOS types**, assigned per map tile.
- Each carries a **`VisibleDistance`** that *clamps* sight for anyone standing in it, overriding the
  zone default (GB_avp_hw ships a 4 / 8 / 12 / 16-block ladder), plus a **`Solid`** flag.
- Each carries an **8×8 `SeeTypeN` matrix**: whether someone in type X can see into type N — so
  visibility between two spaces can be **asymmetric**.

This gives you, as pure level data and with no per-room scripting:

- **A corridor that is genuinely claustrophobic** — 64 px of sight while the open map runs 4000.
- **A doorway you can watch out of but not into** — the ambush primitive, expressed as one matrix
  cell.
- **Graduated interiors** — a lobby that sees further than a service passage, tuned by a designer
  painting tiles rather than by an engineer writing occlusion code.

War World is 3D, so you also have real occlusion available — which makes this *more* attractive, not
less: real geometry handles what you can see *past*, and the LOS type handles how far you can see
*at all*, which is the knob that actually decides whether a room feels tight. **They compose.** And
pair it with the per-class override (`LOS Distance` / `LOS Angle` on the vehicle) so a scout genuinely
sees more of a room than a heavy does.

### 7. Terrain as a full combat parameter, not decoration

The engine tags every tile type with, independently:

- **movement** — 10 values per terrain per vehicle (friction, thrust, rotation, top speed, strafe,
  reverse, sprint) — §2
- **energy regen** — `[TerrainN] EnergyRate`
- **firing cost** — `terrain0EnergyCost … terrain15EnergyCost`, **per weapon**
- **healing / repair** — `HealthRate`, `RepairRate`
- **sight** — the LOS type above

So a single room can be authored as: slow to cross, expensive to shoot in, draining your shield,
and blind past four blocks. **That is an encounter, written entirely in level data.** For a game
whose strength is enclosed spaces, this is the highest-leverage system in the whole engine, and it
costs one lookup table.

### 8. Ricochet count as a per-weapon integer, and it is what separates indoor from outdoor guns

`Wall Bounces` / `Floor Bounces` as a field, not a physics flag. Bullets 0, plasma 1–2, grenade
launcher 5/5. **This one integer is what makes a weapon an indoor weapon** — a projectile that bounces
is a projectile that works around a corner, and it gives interior fights an indirect-fire layer that
open ground does not have.

### 9. No friendly fire, but friendly bodies still block shots

> *"if your shot hits an ally it will be blocked by their body, which somewhat limits the amount of
> Bullet Hell projectile spam possible in team games."*

Cheap to implement, and it is the rule that stops a corridor becoming an undifferentiated wall of
team DPS. Formation matters; nobody gets punished for a teammate's mistake. In a game about doorways
this is nearly free value.

### 10. Range as `velocity × lifetime`. Delete range falloff.

Two numbers per weapon, and you get a 16× range spread (5.8 m flamethrower → 327 m rail sniper) with
no arbitrary cutoffs, no damage-over-distance curve, and a projectile whose behaviour at its
maximum range is *visibly* the same as at point blank — it just stops existing. Players can see and
learn every weapon's reach.

### 11. Movement gates instead of accuracy penalties — and no move-spread at all

Infantry has **no move-accuracy field at all** — confirmed by grepping the whole projectile schema
(§2). Your aim is identical sprinting and standing. In a game whose survival model is "never stop
moving", a spread penalty would have fought the core loop every second of every fight, and they
simply did not ship one.

What they shipped instead is a hard gate: `cannot fire while moving` on the heaviest weapons. That is
better than a spreading reticle in three ways — it is binary, it is **readable by the enemy at a
glance**, and it converts the biggest gun into a stationary target.
TV Tropes calls the heavy MG *"a death sentence against any decent opponents"* — that is a designer
succeeding, not failing, because the weapon is genuinely dominant when the situation earns it.

Pair it with **weight as the movement limiter** rather than a stamina bar. Encumbrance makes every
loadout choice a movement choice, it is always visible, and there is no meter to babysit.

### 12. Summon, deny-summon, and brown-out the denial

The three-layer objective system in §6 is the best thing about Infantry's team play and it is almost
free once you have energy:

- Support classes can **teleport teammates to their position** — one survivor inside the base is a
  spawn point
- **Teleport Disruptors** (carried and deployable) create a denial bubble — this is what a base
  defence *is*
- **Draining a defender to zero switches his Disruptor off** — you win the objective by browning
  someone out, not by killing them

That third rung is the one to make sure you build. It turns a defensive structure into something with
a *power supply you can attack*, and it gives the zero-damage utility weapons a reason to exist.

### 13. Two energy consumables per life, and death is a resupply

**Exactly two Energizers, 200 each, 1.5 s to use.** Not a regenerating charge, not three. That cap is
what turns energy from a bar into a decision, and it is why the playbook could write *"don't plan on
living beyond each 'play'."*

Then make dying cheap and the spawn point a shop — *"Death is just another way to get to the Dropship
without wasting energy."* A short, forgiving death loop is what let Infantry's fights be aggressive
and its assaults be repeatable. If War World's enclosed fights are its strength, the thing you want
is players willing to walk into the room.

### 14. Six fixed damage channels, zone-renamable

The engine hard-codes **exactly six** damage channels — six on every projectile, six on every
armour block — and lets each zone supply **display names** (`[DamageType] Name0..Name5`). So CTF's
"Plasma / Gas-Chemical / Armor Piercing" and BugHunt's "Electronic / Psionic / Bypass" are the *same
slots wearing different labels*, and no maths anywhere changes between them.

This is the right shape for a game that wants more than one setting or more than one map style: the
balance surface stays a fixed 6×N table you can actually reason about, while the fiction stays free.
Add a seventh channel and you have doubled the tuning space forever; rename slot 3 and you have
changed nothing.

Pair it with Infantry's **four-parameter armour** per channel — `Self Ignore`, `Pass Ignore`,
`Self Reduction`, `Pass Damage` — where "self" is damage to the vehicle and "pass" is damage that
reaches the occupant. That second axis is what makes a bunker, a cockpit or a doorway-mounted shield
mean something specific rather than just "more HP".

### 15. EMP that suppresses subsystems independently

`antiEffectsRecharge` / `antiEffectsFire` / `antiEffectsThrust` / `antiEffectsRotate` — four separate
suppressions on one projectile, each with its own radius.

A weapon that stops you **recharging** without stopping you shooting is a completely different threat
from one that stops you **rotating** while you can still run. Both are far more interesting than a
generic stun, and in enclosed spaces they are devastating in ways that read as tactical rather than
unfair: freeze a defender's rotation in a doorway and you have not removed his agency, you have
removed his *facing*.

### 16. Build the tutorial. This is the one thing Infantry never fixed.

Every negative review, and several positive ones, say the same thing: bad UI, no tutorial, twenty-five
years of veterans, no way in. **brized** — one of the game's most articulate defenders — asked for
*"a 'START HERE' zone with some tutorial stages built-in... Duck Game does a good job of this with
small stages that teach you techniques through the challenge they present."*

The mechanics in this file are the reason people played for 25 years. The onboarding is the reason
there are only fifty of them left.
