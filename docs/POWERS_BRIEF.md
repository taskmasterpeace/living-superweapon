Power System Expansion Brief
Read-Aloud Edition
Executive Summary
This plan is grounded in what the engine can actually do.
The engine already supports twenty-six core ability types, a strong set of projectile modifiers, several movement systems, and five combat statuses. That means twenty new powers can be created as pure data. Another twenty require small, contained engine additions. The final twenty require genuinely new systems.
The biggest risk is not a lack of power ideas. The biggest risk is visual collision.
Without a strict visual language, this catalog becomes sixty differently colored versions of the same beam, orb, trail, and explosion. Every power therefore needs a recognizable casting pose, shape, movement pattern, impact, and residue. Color should reinforce the identity, not carry it by itself.
Part One: What the Engine Already Supports
The engine currently has twenty-six ability types:
Melee. Rush. Projectile. Volley. Beam. Cone. Charge. Growing orb. Teleport. Phase. Dash. Summon. Construct. Buff. Tentacle. Portal. Bow. Quiver. Rifle. Face bomb. Nova. Mind control. Grapple. Mine. Life drain. And meteor.
Projectile modifiers already include:
Boomerang behavior. Gravity arcs. Homing. Piercing. Shock. Payloads. Bullet or ballistic behavior. Blade behavior. Canisters. Face-mounted firing. Arming delays. Siphoning. And selectable damage types.
Movement technology already includes:
Flight tiers. Individual fly speed. Cruise flight. Gliders. Grapnels and ledge hanging. Melee pacing. And power charging.
The engine also already recognizes five statuses:
Frozen. Staggered. Gas. Drain. And mind control.
This creates three clear development tiers.
Tier One is definition work.
Tier Two is narrow engine-extension work.
Tier Three is new architecture.
Part Two: The Visual Identity System
Before adding more powers, every ability should receive a visual profile.
That profile should define seven things:

1. Source. Where does the effect originate? The eyes, chest, hands, weapon, ground, sky, or surrounding space?
2. Silhouette. Is the effect a line, disc, fan, cone, wall, ring, orb, tendril, column, or cloud?
3. Motion. Does it travel straight, arc, spiral, return, branch, fall, expand, pull inward, or erupt upward?
4. Impact. Does it puncture, slice, shatter, implode, explode, freeze, deform, or launch?
5. Residue. Does it leave frost, smoke, fire, cracks, a crater, a glyph, scattered metal, or nothing?
6. Material. Does it look physical, technological, elemental, psychic, mystical, biological, gravitational, or sonic?
7. Status tell. How does the player immediately understand what happened to the target?

No two powers should match on more than three of those seven traits.
Visual Families
Physical powers should use real weight, sparks, fragments, dust, recoil, and minimal glow.
Technology powers should use precise lines, targeting brackets, segmented lights, controlled geometry, and mechanical movement.
Elemental powers should use turbulent volume, environmental reactions, and persistent residue.
Psychic and gravity powers should use distortion, refraction, inward movement, stretched particles, and unnatural silence.
Mystical powers should use glyphs, asymmetry, impossible movement, and effects that do not obey ordinary physics.
Biological powers should use tendrils, pulses, elastic movement, uneven shapes, and organic rhythms.
Sonic powers should remain mostly transparent. Their force should become visible through pressure rings, dust, clothing, glass, smoke, and environmental movement.
Status Language
Frozen should always grow upward from the feet using angular crystals.
Stagger should use an offset double-image, broken posture, and asymmetrical motion.
Gas should linger as a diffuse cloud and trigger coughing or choking body language.
Drain should always move particles inward, from the victim toward the attacker.
Mind control should use a visible eye, halo, tether, or head-level signal.
Sleep should use slow downward movement, softened posture, and rounded visual shapes.
Blindness should obscure sight through smoke, a blocked-eye symbol, or interrupted targeting information.
Part Three: Tier One Powers
These are pure data additions. They require new kit entries but no new engine systems.
1. Web Line
Inspired by Spider-Man.
Use the grapple type with a faster reel speed and low energy cost.
Visually, the line should be a twisted two-strand cord instead of a generic cable. The anchor point should form a sticky white splat. Small tension pulses should travel along the strand while the player reels forward.
The casting silhouette should be a fast, one-arm wrist snap.
2. Optic Blast
Inspired by Cyclops.
Use a thin beam with high damage per second and no charge period.
The beam should originate only from the face. It should have a razor-thin ruby exterior and a white-hot center. Impacts should produce flat, rectangular shards of light instead of circular explosions.
Do not give it a glowing charge orb. Its identity is immediate, precise, and relentless.
3. Card Barrage
Inspired by Gambit.
Use a volley with blade behavior and a magenta detonation palette.
Each projectile should remain visibly shaped like a playing card. The cards should tumble with sharp corners catching the light. Their trails should form narrow ribbons, and their detonations should bloom in diamond shapes.
Small card fragments should remain visible for a moment after impact.
4. Pumpkin Bombs
Inspired by Green Goblin.
Use a projectile with canister behavior and a gravity-driven lob.
The bomb should have a readable carved face, blinking eyes, and an arcing smoke trail. It should tumble heavily rather than fly like a missile.
The explosion should open like orange and green fire petals, leaving scorched fragments of the pumpkin shell.
5. Sonic Scream
Inspired by Black Canary and Banshee.
Use a cone with enormous push force and low direct damage.
The attack should create transparent compression rings rather than a glowing energy cone. Hair, clothing, smoke, paper, dust, and nearby debris should react before enemies are launched.
Glass and reflective surfaces should briefly vibrate or fracture at the edges.
6. Ground Slam
Inspired by Hulk and Doomsday.
Use a nova with strong vertical knock-up and a shockwave.
The character’s body hitting the ground should be the center of the effect. Cracks should race outward before the shock ring arrives. Heavy pieces of debris should lift vertically, while lighter dust expands horizontally.
The final visual should be a crater, not a glowing circle.
7. Chest Unibeam
Inspired by Iron Man.
Use a charge that releases a beam-speed orb with high charge power.
The chest aperture should expand through several concentric rings. The projectile should have a thick white core surrounded by a controlled gold or character-specific shell.
Its impact should burn a narrow tunnel through smoke and leave a circular scorch pattern.
8. Returning Shield
Inspired by Captain America.
Use a projectile with boomerang and blade behavior.
The painted face of the shield should remain readable while it spins. Alternating views of the front and metal back should produce a rhythmic flashing effect.
The trail should be a metallic crescent. The return should end with a sharp visible catch animation rather than having the shield disappear into the hand.
9. Ice Slick Trap
Inspired by Iceman and Captain Cold.
Use a mine with a freezing payload and slow arming time.
The trap should spread across the ground as reflective frost rather than appearing as a standard mine. Thin ice branches should creep outward from the center.
Low mist should hug the surface. When triggered, the ice should surge upward around the target’s feet before shattering.
10. Hellfire Chains
Inspired by Ghost Rider.
Use a tentacle with a fire palette and drag-in behavior.
The chain should be made from black iron segments with orange heat visible inside the joints. It should move like a striking snake rather than a straight grappling hook.
When wrapped around a target, embers should race down the links. Dragging the enemy inward should leave a spiral of soot.
11. Twin Pistols
Inspired by Punisher and Deadshot.
Use two rifle slots with one-handed behavior enabled for both.
The left and right pistols should alternate their muzzle flashes clearly. Recoil should shift the upper body from side to side, creating a recognizable firing rhythm.
Brass should eject in two separate arcs. Tracers should remain thin and physical, without excessive glow.
12. Poison Dart Fan
Inspired by Elektra and Cheshire.
Use a volley with a gas payload.
The fan should contain clearly visible needle-shaped darts rather than small generic projectiles. Their trails should resemble thin vapor threads.
The impact should create several small toxic blossoms that merge into a larger cloud. The poison palette should remain yellow-green so it does not resemble ice, sleep, or mind control.
13. Kinetic Absorption Stance
Inspired by Bishop and Sebastian Shaw.
Use a buff that combines healing and damage multiplication.
Incoming impacts should collapse into the body as concentric rings. Instead of sparks flying away, particles should be pulled inward.
Stored energy should visibly climb from the torso into the arms and shoulders. As the stored multiplier increases, the character should appear denser and more energized.
14. Shadow Step
Inspired by Nightcrawler.
Use a teleport with a smoke palette and a short cooldown.
The character should collapse into a flat black silhouette before breaking into smoke. Reappearance should occur through a thin vertical tear rather than a circular portal.
The entrance and exit should leave brief soot-like footprints or claw-shaped smoke marks.
15. Stone Volley
Inspired by Terra.
Use a volley with gravity, shock, and a rock-based palette.
Every stone should have a different silhouette. Some should be flat, some round, and some jagged. They should travel in dusty ballistic arcs rather than energy-straight lines.
Impacts should produce chips, powder, and localized cracks instead of fire.
16. Orbital Lance
Inspired by a heat-vision dive from Zod.
Use a meteor with a count of one and a very large single impact radius.
The ground should first receive a narrow warning mark from the sky. A thin targeting line should appear before the full column arrives.
The final strike should descend vertically, overwhelm the warning line, and leave a fused or glassed crater rather than a standard explosion.
17. Life Leech Touch
Inspired by Rogue and Morbius.
Use life drain with short range and a strong siphon value.
The victim should visibly lose color while the attacker gains saturation and brightness. Particles should travel backward from the victim’s body into the attacker’s hand.
The effect must remain short and intimate. It should look like contact-based theft, not a long-range beam.
18. Drone Swarm
Inspired by Blue Beetle and Vulture.
Use summon with four low-damage drones.
The four drones should enter and maintain a readable diamond or cross formation. Each drone should occupy a different position rather than stacking into one visual cluster.
Their targeting lights should synchronize before firing. When repositioning, they should orbit the player instead of teleporting between formation points.
19. Force Wall
Inspired by Invisible Woman.
Use a wall construct.
The wall should remain mostly transparent, but its boundary should be readable through refraction. Four anchor points or corners should define its exact dimensions.
Every impact should create a localized ripple or hexagonal stress pattern. The entire wall should not flash every time one bullet strikes it.
20. Berserker Rush
Inspired by Wolverine.
Use a rush with eight hits, slash damage, and a relatively weak finisher.
The character should lower into a predatory forward posture. Each hit should generate a short claw-shaped afterimage rather than a full explosion.
The repeated hits should form a readable rhythm. The final strike should create a heavy skid, debris spray, and long slash mark across the ground.
Part Four: Tier Two Powers
These need a small amount of coding. Each requires approximately one new flag, one new status, or one contained subsystem adjustment.
1. Sleeping Arrows
Inspired by Green Arrow’s tranquilizer arrows.
Add a sleep status. The affected character becomes uncontrolled, displays a clear sleeping tell, and wakes when damaged. The status can be delivered through a sleep payload on arrows or darts.
The projectile should have a rounded tranquilizer capsule instead of a pointed combat head. The target’s posture should slowly collapse rather than instantly freezing.
Use pale gold or lavender rings and three slowly orbiting dots above the target. Do not reuse poison-green or ice-blue effects.
2. Smoke Bomb Blindness
Inspired by Batman.
Add a blind payload that disrupts artificial intelligence targeting while inside the cloud.
The smoke should be dense, oily, and layered. Characters inside it should appear only as broken silhouettes.
A blocked-eye symbol should appear above affected fighters. Enemy shots should visibly deviate, strike nearby cover, or pass through incorrect targeting positions.
3. Ricochet Rounds
Inspired by Bullseye and Domino.
Add a bounce count to projectiles so they can reflect from cover a defined number of times.
Each tracer should be divided into straight segments. Every rebound should create a sharp spark and a visible directional kink.
Brief numbered pips or marks could appear on the struck surface, making the remaining bounce count readable without relying on the HUD.
4. Sticky Bombs
Inspired by the weapon designs from Batman Returns.
Add a stick flag that attaches the projectile to a victim or surface before detonation.
The bomb should open small mechanical clamps on contact. Its blinking fuse should accelerate as detonation approaches.
When attached to a moving target, the bomb must remain clearly visible and should conform to the target’s surface instead of floating beside the body.
5. Chain Lightning
Inspired by Storm and Sith-style lightning.
Add an on-hit chain property with a maximum number of targets and a chaining range.
The first arc should be thickest. Each additional jump should become thinner and less stable.
Targets should flash in sequence rather than simultaneously. Branch points should remain visible for a fraction of a second, allowing the player to understand the path the lightning followed.
6. Black Hole Round
Inspired by gravity weapons and singularity attacks.
Combine a growing orb with a pulling field.
The center should appear almost black, surrounded by a warped ring that bends the background. Dust, smoke, shell casings, and debris should curve inward before enemies move.
The final effect should implode. Avoid turning it into another outward explosion.
7. Vampiric Aura
Inspired by supernatural enemies associated with Blade.
Add an aura that applies repeated siphon ticks to nearby enemies while a buff is active.
The aura should remain close to the ground as a dark crimson circle. Thin veins of energy should stretch from nearby enemies toward the caster.
The caster’s shadow should deepen and spread as more targets are drained.
8. Ground Spikes
Inspired by Doomsday and Toph.
Create a cone variation that raises temporary cover colliders.
Cracks should race forward along the ground before the spikes emerge. The spikes should rise sequentially rather than all at once.
Their material should match the environment. Asphalt should produce broken road and rebar. Dirt should produce stone and earth. Interior floors should produce fractured tile and concrete.
9. Decoy Hologram
Inspired by Loki and Mysterio.
Spawn an untargetable copy using the player’s mesh and cause enemies to retarget it.
The decoy should have slight color separation around its edges, subtle scanlines, and occasional frame-skips.
It should cast no proper contact shadow and produce no dust or physical footprints. When struck, it should glitch rather than react like a body.
10. Overwatch Turret Buff
Inspired by engineer archetypes.
Allow a turret to inherit the owner’s stat-sheet multipliers.
The turret should adopt the owner’s emblem, accent pattern, or projected sigil. A visible energy conduit should periodically connect the owner and turret.
Targeting lasers should become brighter and more deliberate while the inherited buff is active.
11. Blade Cyclone
Inspired by Baraka and Wolverine-style spinning attacks.
Combine a nova with slash damage and a persistent one-second damage tick.
The effect should form a serrated metallic halo around the character. It should use repeated blade glints and slash arcs rather than a spherical energy field.
Dust and loose debris should spiral inward toward the rotating center.
12. Magnet Pull
Inspired by Magneto.
Create a cone that pulls thrown props and vehicles toward the player.
Eligible metal objects should first vibrate and receive a thin polarity outline. They should then follow curved trajectories into the pull.
Nonmetal objects should remain unaffected. This contrast is essential because it makes the power feel magnetic rather than telekinetic.
13. Frost Nova
Inspired by Sub-Zero.
Combine a nova with the existing frozen status.
A low ring of ice should race outward along the floor. When it reaches a target, crystals should grow upward from the feet and lock the body in place.
The effect should use fixed radial symmetry, sharp crystals, cold mist, and visible breath. It should not resemble the irregular spreading shape of the Ice Slick Trap.
14. Adrenaline Surge
Inspired by Bane’s venom.
Create a buff that increases performance but drains health every second.
The character’s veins or armor channels should pulse in time with a visible heartbeat. Heat haze and vapor should rise from the shoulders.
Each health-loss tick should briefly dim the glow, showing that the body is paying for the increased power.
15. Sniper Stance
Inspired by Deadshot.
Add a zoom state to rifles, with slower fire, increased range, and a tighter crosshair.
The strongest visual element should be stillness. The character’s posture should lock, breathing movement should reduce, and the weapon lens should produce one controlled glint.
Shots should create a single needle-thin tracer. Avoid large muzzle flashes or explosive impacts unless the ammunition itself requires them.
16. Seeker Micro-Missiles
Inspired by Iron Man.
Expose the existing homing property to volley attacks.
Missile pods should open before firing. The missiles should launch as a cluster and then separate toward different target points.
Their trails should corkscrew slightly. The targeting bracket should divide into several smaller lock points before launch.
17. Phase Walk
Inspired by Kitty Pryde.
Extend the existing phase ability so it can pass through interior walls.
The body should become glass-like, partially transparent, and rim-lit. It should not become smoke, because smoke is already the language of Shadow Step.
When the character passes through a wall, the surface should ripple like disturbed liquid and then immediately return to normal.
18. Counter Stance
Inspired by Batman combat systems.
Create a buff window in which the next incoming melee attack triggers an automatic riposte.
A circular timing ring should contract around the defender’s forearms or torso. As an attacker commits, the attacking limb should briefly highlight.
The successful counter should include a very short pose hold at the moment of reversal, creating a readable martial-arts punctuation without relying on a giant effect.
19. Grapple Slam
Inspired by Attack on Titan.
Create a grapnel variation that attaches to an enemy and reels that enemy toward the player.
The cable should be heavier and more visible than the Web Line. A tension pulse should move from the player to the victim when the pull begins.
The victim should follow a readable swing arc. The slam should end in a directional crater, followed by the cable snapping backward.
20. Air Superiority
Inspired by Hawkman.
Add bonus melee damage and knockback against airborne targets.
An airborne enemy should receive a subtle wind-shaped targeting bracket. Attacks should create downward-curving slash trails.
When the victim hits the ground, the impact should form a compressed cone of air beneath them, reinforcing the vertical nature of the attack.
Part Five: Tier Three Powers
Each of these requires a new engine system. Each system should unlock an entire family of future kits rather than serving only one character.
1. Weather Command
Inspired by Storm.
Create a real weather layer containing rain, wind, cloud density, and lightning that can affect fighters, pedestrians, and the environment.
The caster should receive a localized cloud crown or rotating pressure pattern overhead. Global weather changes should build gradually instead of switching instantly.
Rain should bend with wind. Lightning should illuminate whole building silhouettes. Strong wind should move debris, clothing, smoke, projectiles, and vegetation.
2. Size Change
Inspired by Ant-Man and Giant-Man.
Create scale-aware hitboxes, mass, camera positioning, door measurements, and movement physics.
The environment must communicate scale. Dust, footsteps, camera height, object size, and impact strength should all change together.
Growing should create a heavy outward mass wave. Shrinking should pull nearby particles inward and reveal normally invisible environmental detail.
This system is expensive because it touches nearly everything.
3. Time Dilation Field
Inspired by Quicksilver scenes.
Create local time-scale bubbles using the world’s existing slow-motion hooks.
The field should appear as a refractive sphere. Motion trails inside it should stretch, while smoke, shell casings, rain, and debris appear suspended.
Characters crossing the boundary should visibly compress or extend their movement trails, making the transition readable.
4. Duplicates
Inspired by Multiple Man.
Create true artificial-intelligence clones that share a single health pool.
Clones should split from the original as offset afterimages that become solid. Each clone should have a small number or shoulder marking for tracking.
Whenever the shared health pool changes, a quick pulse should travel through every active duplicate.
5. Possession
Inspired by Deadman.
Create full control transfer into another body, including movement, attacks, camera behavior, and HUD information.
A spectral version of the player should leave the original body and stream into the target. The target’s eyes or head-level aura should change immediately.
The HUD should transform rather than simply swap instantly. The abandoned body should remain visibly vulnerable or inactive.
6. Elasticity
Inspired by Mister Fantastic.
Create stretchable limb rendering and melee reach that changes dynamically.
Limbs should retain believable volume while stretching. Hands and feet should not become tiny points at the end of thin lines.
Attacks should follow curved rubber-like paths. Retraction should create visible snapback waves through the limb.
This is another high-risk system because it touches animation, collision, camera framing, and melee timing.
7. Invisibility
Inspired by Invisible Woman.
Create a rendering state and an artificial-intelligence perception layer.
The character should not become perfectly invisible to the controlling player. Use refraction, edge distortion, displaced rain, disturbed dust, and subtle footprints.
Enemies should lose target lock, crosshairs should break, and artificial-intelligence perception should depend on movement, proximity, and environmental disturbance.
8. Wall-Crawling
Inspired by Spider-Man.
Create a surface-locomotion state that works on building faces and other valid surfaces.
Hands and feet should display brief adhesion pulses when contacting the wall. The camera horizon should rotate smoothly as the character transitions between ground, wall, and ceiling.
The player’s posture should remain aligned to the surface rather than using a normal running animation rotated sideways.
9. Telekinesis: Grab Anything
Inspired by Jean Grey.
Create free-aim selection, carrying, rotating, and throwing for props, debris, and fighters.
Selected objects should receive a clear outline and orbiting axis rings. A faint projected trajectory should show where the object will travel.
Telekinesis should look clean and controlled. This separates it from Magnet Pull, which only affects metal and produces chaotic convergence.
10. Portal Pairs
Inspired by Portal and Doctor Strange.
Create linked gates that redirect movement and projectiles in both directions.
The two portals should use opposite edge rotations and distinct warm and cool identities. The destination must be visible through the opening.
Projectiles should preserve speed and direction correctly. Their trail should visibly bend through the portal instead of stopping and restarting.
11. Terrain Reshaping
Inspired by Avatar-style bending.
Allow runtime modification of cover and ground, including walls, trenches, ramps, and street deformation.
The ground should first display contour lines showing the proposed shape. Material should then rise in visible layers or geological strata.
Changes should leave persistent dust, cracks, loose material, and readable collision boundaries.
12. Symbiote Consume
Inspired by Venom.
Allow the player to steal one ability slot from a victim for the duration of the match.
Organic tendrils should attach to the target and pull a visible representation of the power toward the caster.
The stolen ability’s HUD slot should be wrapped in a black organic frame. The caster should temporarily gain a small visual trait associated with the stolen ability.
13. Power Mimicry
Inspired by Rogue and Amazo.
Copy the target’s complete kit temporarily.
This must look different from Symbiote Consume. Mimicry should use a clean scanning lattice, geometric bands, and controlled transformation.
The user should gain simplified costume accents or aura elements from the copied target. Copied effects can receive a faint double-outline to show that they are imitations.
14. Summon Rideable
Inspired by Ghost Rider’s motorcycle and rideable dragons.
Create mounts with their own movement kits, collision, health rules, and controls.
Every mount needs a signature entrance and mounting animation. A motorcycle may tear through a burning portal. A dragon may descend through clouds or unfold from magical energy.
Mounted movement must have its own wake, camera behavior, and HUD identity.
15. Energy Shield Bubble
Inspired by Green Lantern.
Create a projectable dome that blocks hostile fire while allowing allied fire to leave.
The dome should remain readable through refraction and a bright internal edge. Impacts should create local hexagonal stress patterns.
Allied projectiles exiting the dome should briefly open small light petals or windows in the surface. Enemy attacks should flatten against it.
16. X-Ray and Thermal Sense
Inspired by Superman.
Create vision modes that override canopy and interior cutaway restrictions.
Thermal vision should display heat-based silhouettes and recent heat trails.
X-ray vision should emphasize structural edges, skeletal forms, machinery, and hidden objects.
The two modes must have different visual grammars. They should not be the same outline effect with two different colors.
17. Regeneration Factor
Inspired by Wolverine and Doomsday.
Create a death-defiance state in which a knockout becomes a downed regeneration period unless the fighter is finished.
Regeneration should pulse outward from the heart or central body. Wounds, broken armor, and damaged materials should visibly close in reverse.
A clear finisher window should surround the downed character so opponents understand that the knockout is not yet permanent.
18. Banishment
Inspired by Shadow King.
Send a target into a pocket dimension for a fixed period.
The target should be enclosed by a vertical geometric or shadow cage that collapses into a single point.
A visible return scar should remain at the departure location. The pocket dimension should have an unmistakable horizon, sky, and material language so it does not look like an ordinary teleport.
19. Gravity Inversion Zone
Inspired by Graviton.
Reverse gravity within a defined area so objects become airborne and ceilings can act as floors.
Dust, rain, clothing, and loose debris should react first, warning players before their bodies lift.
The zone boundary should contain particles that appear to fall upward. The camera should roll only after the controlled character commits to the new gravity direction.
20. Interplanetary Flight
Inspired by Superman leaving Earth.
Extend flight beyond the city and into a larger travel layer.
The player should move through visible atmospheric stages. The sky should darken, the horizon should curve, the atmosphere should form a bright rim, and stars should become stable.
High-speed exit should produce a plasma sheath and compressed wake before transitioning into the system map.
Part Six: Flight Speed and Afterburners
Flight speed is already becoming a real character statistic.
The engine currently has per-character fly speeds ranging roughly from one point zero eight to one point three. Individual definitions can also override fly speed.
Holding Shift while airborne activates universal cruise. Cruise currently provides approximately one point five times speed while applying a small energy drain.
The next addition should be an afterburner definition containing two values:
A speed multiplier.
And an energy cost per second.
Designated high-performance fliers, such as Sol, Majesty, Torch, and Nova-class characters, should receive an afterburner around two point one times normal flight speed.
That creates three meaningful flight classes.
Slow Levitators
Slow levitators should remain upright and relaxed. They should have little or no contrail. Dust, clothing, and loose particles should subtly rise around them.
Their movement should feel controlled rather than aerodynamic.
Standard Fliers
Standard fliers should lean into their travel direction. They should produce one narrow wake or a pair of short pressure ribbons.
Cruise should lengthen the wake and tighten the character’s body posture.
Afterburner Fliers
Afterburner users should enter a spear-like flight posture. The effect should begin with a compression ring, followed by a long wake, heat distortion, and a visible reduction in control at extreme speed.
When the energy tank runs dry, the wake should break apart rather than simply disappearing.
Do not give every afterburner the same generic blue trail.
Sol could use a gold corona surrounding a white-hot core.
Majesty could use silver and violet shock diamonds.
Torch could produce turbulent fire, ember shedding, and a heat-distorted tail.
Nova could use a compact stellar core with star-like particles dragged into the wake.
The movement system can be shared. The wake material, posture, and acceleration profile should carry the character identity.
Part Seven: City-to-City Travel
City-to-city movement should use the map screen, not continuous raw flight.
Simulating hundreds of kilometers of empty terrain would consume development time while creating little meaningful gameplay.
When a capable flier climbs above the Clouds altitude band and continues holding the burner, the game should offer a Depart action.
Depart opens the world map.
The Atlas system already contains countries, cities, population information, and climate information. The player selects a destination from that existing structure.
Travel time should equal distance divided by effective flight speed.
Afterburner use reduces travel time but consumes energy.
Arrival loads the destination city plan and begins with the cinematic introduction system. The country or location identifies itself as the new city appears.
The in-city GPS and the world map must remain separate tools.
The GPS handles streets, districts, objectives, and local navigation.
The world map handles travel between cities, countries, planets, and systems.
Visually, the map route should inherit the traveler’s wake identity. A fire-based flier should draw a burning route. A cosmic flier should draw a stellar route. A technological flier should draw a precise segmented route.
Destination previews should show climate, local time, skyline silhouette, and major environmental conditions before the player commits.
Part Eight: Space and the Altitude Ladder
The altitude system is already data-driven.
Current bands include Ground, Building, Sky, and Clouds. The same model can extend downward into water and upward into space.
Add Orbit above Clouds.
Only afterburner-class fliers should be able to force their way through the upper atmosphere. As altitude increases, atmospheric density should fall and oxygen or energy drain should rise.
The visual transition should happen in stages.
First, clouds fall below the player.
Second, the sky darkens.
Third, the horizon curves.
Fourth, the atmosphere becomes a thin illuminated rim.
Fifth, stars stop moving with the camera and become stable navigation references.
From orbit, the world map zooms out by one level.
The progression becomes:
City.
Country.
Planet.
System.
And eventually interstellar space.
A planet can use the same data model as a country. It contains its own city plans, climates, sky palettes, gravity values, and atmosphere values.
Mars settlements can be authored through the same basic planning system as Miami. The difference comes from environmental data, not from an entirely separate map architecture.
Leaving the solar system uses the same map again at a larger zoom level.
The data model remains consistent. Only the map’s zoom stack, destination hierarchy, gravity, and atmosphere change.

According to the current build status, the working tree already contains several visual improvements.
Batarangs and Stormcall’s axe now appear as spinning steel blades.
Fragmentation and gas shells now appear as tumbling canisters with visible fuse lights.
Arrows now produce a pale air wake that scales with speed.
Drone kills now correctly credit the drone’s owner, resolving the undefined Stands lower-third problem.
The afterburner block is fully specified and is ready to be added to the section of `entity.js` where cruise flight currently lives.

Part Ten: Recommended Development Sequence
Phase Zero: Build the Visual Contract
Before producing dozens of effects, add a data-driven visual profile to every ability definition.
At minimum, define:
Source.
Shape.
Trail.
Impact.
Residue.
Material family.
And status tell.
This is higher leverage than adding another individual power. Without it, the visual system will become a collection of one-off exceptions.
Phase One: Ship Tier One
Tier One is available immediately because it uses systems that already exist.
Build these powers in small batches and test them in combat together. Do not develop all twenty in isolation and assume they remain readable once layered on top of one another.
Phase Two: Build Sleeping Arrows
Sleeping Arrows should be the first Tier Two power.
It requires one new status, one clear HUD tell, and one wake-on-damage rule. More importantly, it validates the entire payload pathway for future statuses.
Once sleep works correctly on arrows and darts, the same delivery architecture becomes reusable.
Phase Three: Expand the Status and Chaining Lanes
Build Frost Nova next because frozen status already exists.
Then build Chain Lightning because it validates chained target selection and shock presentation.
Then build Smoke Bomb Blindness because it tests artificial-intelligence perception and targeting disruption.
These three additions cover status application, chained hits, and perception interference.
Phase Four: Expand Projectile Behavior
Add Ricochet Rounds.
Then Sticky Bombs.
Then expose homing to volleys for Seeker Micro-Missiles.
This creates reusable projectile behavior instead of isolated character-specific code.
Phase Five: Build Afterburner and World Travel
Complete afterburner flight.
Add the Clouds departure gate.
Connect departure to the Atlas map.
Add travel-time calculations.
Add destination arrival cinematics.
This should happen before major Tier Three experimentation because it connects the existing city, climate, flight, and cinematic systems.
Phase Six: Make Interplanetary Flight the First Tier Three System
Interplanetary Flight is the Tier Three feature that pays off the largest amount of existing work.
It uses flight speed.
Afterburners.
Altitude bands.
Atlas data.
Map zoom levels.
Climate fields.
Gravity values.
Atmosphere values.
And arrival cinematics.
It is not merely another power. It turns several disconnected systems into one coherent world structure.
Phase Seven: Build High-Leverage Tier Three Systems
After Interplanetary Flight, prioritize Portal Pairs and Telekinesis.
Portal Pairs unlock new movement, puzzles, projectiles, traps, and combat interactions.
Telekinesis unlocks interaction with props, debris, vehicles, and fighters.
Invisibility should follow once artificial-intelligence perception has already been touched by Smoke Bomb Blindness.
Systems to Delay
Size Change, Elasticity, Terrain Reshaping, and Gravity Inversion should remain near the back of the roadmap.
They sound exciting, but each one touches physics, collision, animation, camera behavior, artificial intelligence, level design, and multiplayer synchronization.
Building them too early would be a classic case of chasing spectacle while destabilizing the foundation.
Final Visual Readability Tests
Every power should pass five tests before shipping.
The Grayscale Test
Remove all color.
The power should still be recognizable from its shape, source, movement, and impact.
The Freeze-Frame Test
Pause the game at any moment during the ability.
A viewer should be able to identify what is happening from one still image.
The Half-Second Test
Show only half a second of the effect.
The player should recognize the power family before seeing the final impact.
The Combat-Chaos Test
Activate four different powers in the same space.
Each effect should remain readable without covering every character, objective, and projectile.
The Status Test
A player should understand whether a target is frozen, asleep, blinded, drained, poisoned, staggered, or mind-controlled without needing to inspect a text label.
The final standard is simple:
A player should see half a second of an effect, with the color removed, and still be able to name the power.
