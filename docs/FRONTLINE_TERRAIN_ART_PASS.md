## Native runtime changes

Four complete project-authored mesa/scarp volumes replace the runtime cliff-panel/cap assembly. Each has one closed crown/scarp/skirt surface and one material, with a cheaper distant LOD. The legacy `buildCliffFormation` export remains for existing diagnostics but is no longer used by the terrain loader. Blender source, export and repeatable authoring script are retained. These are mathematically sculpted meshes with photographed secondary relief, not photogrammetry of whole mesas or claimed manual sculpting.

The follow-up stratification pass replaces the steep-face radial sampling with explicit upper/lower sediment-bed rings connected to the crown and base. This was necessary because sparse radial sampling erased ledges during simplification. The final source uses unequal interrupted beds, broad fault planes, asymmetrical buttresses, and crown-height faults instead of identical horizontal courses. Two intermediate sculpt revisions were rejected during live review (vertical fluting, then overly regular courses) before this source was adopted.

Sandstone albedo, normal and roughness project in world units, preventing texture stretch across differently sized formations. Cover cutaway clones retain their shader callbacks. The native ground combines local photographed gravel and broad mineral/sediment patches; steeper native slopes blend to the same sandstone surface. The photographic sky has separate exposure/chroma calibration; aerial perspective is lighter and warmer near the valley, cooler at distance.

The native heightfield now contains a winding clear valley and connected stepped foothills, with an authored base stored in `_ghBase`. The 130-unit entry/objective pad remains flat and walkable. The renderer, `heightAt`, crater writes and reset all use the same native vertices. An exterior annular mesh shares all 1024 native perimeter vertices and supplies rolling far terrain, leaving the entire crater patch uncovered. The 900-unit combat boundary stays intact. Mesas remain landable native cover.

The subsequent structural composition round intentionally replaces the old radial spire placement. `frontline-layout.js` authors 15 broad formations, 110–220 units wide, in two geological bands on either side of a clear central forward corridor. Near cliffs rise from connected 60–120-unit native shoulders; smaller intermediate formations step toward three distance layers of far topography. Native cover proxies are measured from these new visible volumes, while explicit interior damage/loot budgets retain the existing durability and rubble formulas. The 22 small boulders are deterministically placed outside the clear pad and other cover, then lifted with their collider tops and reset anchors onto the native hillside. Tests now assert the new layout's contracts instead of freezing the obsolete random map coordinates.

Two instanced rubble draws add 1100 sub-one-unit surface chips inside the arena and 650 medium decorative stones outside combat bounds. Near chips settle to `heightAt` after native crater position writes. They do not add collision records. Existing terrain remains 37 rock covers / 55 reskinned objects; parent-owned convoy cover is counted separately.

Native bank contours now retreat irregularly and have transverse 25-unit drainage cuts through the shelves. The cuts are sampled in `_ghBase`, not a visual overlay; tests include rendered-ray/standing-height witnesses through them. Elevated shelf tops and slopes expose the same photographed sandstone with its normal map, while low valley ground retains sediment/gravel. Mid-distance aerial perspective is warmer, with low-lying analytic dust coloration. No renderer, camera, hero, vehicle, or UI changes belong to this terrain follow-up.

The subsequent foothill round adds `frontline-foothills.js`: 20 authored tilted, chamfered near bedrock/fallen-section benches and 12 staggered intermediate/far ridge slabs. Their maximum union is sampled into the same native base before existing drainage cuts and entry-pad clearance. The initial strong slab field and geometric face-normal override were rejected in native review because they made obvious triangular excavation berms. The override is removed and the field is reduced to supporting relief. The two nearest tall formations are intentionally lowered to 175/165 units. A conservative acceleration bound includes the entire bevel tail, with a continuity regression preventing false cliff drops at the culling edge.

The adopted follow-up uses four closed scan-derived talus fragments with cut fracture planes and integral level crowns. Twelve existing boulder records become four three-piece piles; ten small rocks remain. Each fragment retains native cover collision and destruction rather than becoming a floating decorative overlay. Source cap precision is tested to 0.00001 source units and native crown agreement to 0.001 world units. Native projection identified the dominant screen-right wall at world (-310,205); it is moved backward to (-440,420). The negative-X convoy corridor remains reserved. Formation count, 37 rock covers and 55 skins remain unchanged.

The outward-muzzle cover fixture now starts at native ground +8 and aims above an uphill obstruction when needed. This keeps it a cover-contact regression after the parent added real beam/terrain collision; the previous fixed y=8 fixture was underground on hills. Parent-owned projectile/contact, worldImpact and disposal fixes are preserved.

## Source assets / budgets

`public/models/frontline/eroded-mesa-kit.glb`: approximately 1.15 MB, four approximately 11k-triangle near meshes and four approximately 2k-triangle distant LODs. glTF Transform 4.5.0 prunes/deduplicates/welds without a runtime decoder. Added texture files total approximately 3.64 MB. The editable `.blend`, source export, exact commands, license links and detailed methodology are in `assets-src/frontline-mesas/README.md`.

Photographed material sources are Poly Haven CC0: Rock Face (Greg Zaal / Dario Barresi) and Aerial Ground Rock (Rob Tuytel). Source JPEGs and publisher metadata are preserved; the fetch script checks publisher sizes and MD5. Runtime never requests external assets. Portable Blender came from the official Blender release server and lives only under `assets-src/tooling`, with no global installation.

`public/models/frontline/fractured-talus-kit.glb`: approximately 541 KB, four approximately 6k-triangle closed meshes, one material each, no runtime decoder or new textures. Derived from Poly Haven CC0 Namaqualand Boulder 04 by Jenelle van Heerden. Editable Blender source, repeatable authoring/export commands, topology cleanup and license details are in `assets-src/frontline-talus/README.md`. The loader adopts all twelve target skins atomically and preserves late-load ownership/disposal.

The new surfaces remove wrapped square walls, thin separate caps and exposed scan backs. However, the target still has much denser naturally fractured ledges and large talus blocks. Current banks remain comparatively smooth, near cliff masses too uniformly vertical, and far silhouettes sparse. The authored ledges are a partial improvement, not a convincing substitute for the target's complex fractured rock. Atmosphere is analytic rather than volumetric dust. This evidence is not a basis to claim a completed target match or sustained 60 fps.
