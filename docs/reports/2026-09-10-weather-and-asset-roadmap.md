# Weather and asset roadmap — 10 September 2026

Recommendation: expose and polish the existing daylight, rain and storm systems first; follow with one bounded tornado prototype. Pilot anyCreature for a creature, img2threejs for a prop, and CMU for a small human motion set. Aircraft work is explicitly deferred. This is a design and cost assessment only; no runtime implementation or asset installation was performed for this report.

## Existing weather groundwork

- [`Weather`](../../src/engine/systems.js) already provides named states, gradual rain/wind/cloud transitions, a reusable 1,400-point rain buffer, airborne wind forces, lightning and delayed thunder. Commanded lightning can damage through the existing combat pipeline when it has a source owner.
- [`data/weather.js`](../../src/data/weather.js) defines clear, cloudy, rain, storm, hurricane and other states, climate selection, golden-hour colors and material-kind wind drag. A hurricane state already exists; that does not establish a localized tornado. `ai.js` consumes `visMult`, and projectiles consume the wind-force lookup. Do not assume every declared wetness, fog or lighting parameter already has a visible consumer.
- [`world.updateDayNight`](../../src/engine/world.js) advances `dayT` over 240 simulation seconds unless `dayFixed` is set, and updates sky, lighting and emissive details. [`PowerWorld`](../../src/engine/powerworld.js) intentionally pins daylight to `0.2`, restores the prior clock on exit, and gets special lighting intensities in `world.js`. Preserve its bright default; an optional night preset needs coordinated changes in these existing owners.

## Smallest useful sequence

These are preliminary engineering estimates for someone familiar with this codebase, including focused verification. They are planning ranges, not delivery promises; visual quality and unresolved integration issues can expand them.

| Order | Bounded deliverable | Estimated effort | Main cost / acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Day, sunset and readable night presets through the existing clock/lighting owners | 0.5–1.5 days | Little new rendering work; verify fighter silhouettes, HUD, target visibility and restoration on map exit. Keep PowerWorld's current default. |
| 2 | Clear → rain → clear using the existing particle buffer | 1–2 days | Terrain-relative placement, roof coverage and transparency overdraw. Verify at ground, rooftop and flight heights, plus return/reset. |
| 3 | Storm presentation with terrain-correct lightning and thunder | 0.5–1.5 days | Existing VFX/audio reuse; validate strike endpoints, optional damage ownership, flash readability and delayed-audio cleanup. |
| 4 | One short-lived, localized tornado with capped debris and a visible warning radius | 2–5 days | New spatial force field, collision-safe pull/lift, debris budget, escape behavior and teardown. Prove one instance before allowing several. |

Rain needs a concrete audit before being called ready for the present terrain. Its current particles respawn at absolute `y=200–230`, recycle below `y=0`, and center their horizontal respawn around the camera. Lightning starts at `y=0`, with damage at `y=1`. Test hills, depressions, roofs and high flight against the existing terrain/surface queries. Use a bounded camera-local rain volume and cached/coarse surface sampling where possible; avoid adding 1,400 full scene raycasts per frame. Roof occlusion and low-cost splash placement are separate acceptance checks.

For the tornado, reuse weather parameters and existing force/damage/VFX plumbing. Add a finite-radius field with capped inward, tangential and upward forces; preserve physical collision and the material-kind rule that leaves energy projectiles out of wind drag. Start with a small shared-mesh debris pool and one funnel effect. Delay whole-map debris destruction and elaborate volumetric clouds until the single-instance version survives dense combat and match cleanup.

No external weather API, paid asset service or recurring API bill is required for this route. Engineering time, browser GPU/CPU time and QA are the costs. Transparent rain/funnel effects can be expensive through overdraw even with few draw calls. Measure foreground frame time on the same camera and combat scene before/after each preset; particle/debris caps and quality scaling should be informed by those measurements. No GPU performance gain or frame-rate result is claimed here.

## Asset pilots

**anyCreature: first choice for one creature summon or NPC.** The project emits animated, skinned GLB files with vertex colors and baked ambient occlusion; its local compiler does not require a generation API or CUDA. Its output contract specifies metres, +Y up and +Z forward. Pilot one creature with idle, movement and one verified attack; integrate bounds, AI and contact timing explicitly. Generated animation availability is not evidence of combat-ready motion. Budget roughly 1–3 days for a first integrated pilot. The project is MIT-licensed; keep input provenance and distinguish its optional publication consent from local compilation. Agent usage and full visual-QA dependencies remain costs. [Repository](https://github.com/Ariescar/anyCreature), [output contract](https://github.com/Ariescar/anyCreature/blob/main/docs/OUTPUT_CONTRACT.md).

**img2threejs: first choice for one distinctive procedural prop or weapon.** It rebuilds a reference as a TypeScript `THREE.Group` factory plus a JSON specification, fitting the project's procedural approach. The Python core uses the standard library; the host agent supplies visual judgment and browser review. Budget roughly 0.5–2 days for a bounded prop pilot, including material/draw-call review and mount/collider integration. The core is Apache-2.0; use references whose reproduction rights are understood. Its character plugin says base reconstructions are unrigged and four rig checks currently lack input producers, so do not promise a ready-to-fight hero from a single image. [Repository](https://github.com/img2threejs/img2threejs), [character-plugin limitations](https://github.com/img2threejs/plugin-character).

**CMU: curated human motion, with a retargeting budget.** Start with a few boxing/kick clips, not the full library. Subject 135 includes front kick 04, roundhouse 07 and side kick 11. Bake chosen motion into the existing visual pose bridge in [`authored-pose.js`](../../src/engine/authored-pose.js), remove motion that would drive the physics root, and align contact/recovery timing with gameplay. Skeleton scale, foot contact and noisy extremities need review. Budget roughly 1–3 days to establish the first small import batch; reuse may reduce later clip cost. CMU permits inclusion in commercial products but prohibits resale of the dataset, including converted forms. Retain source IDs and terms with derived clips. [CMU database and use terms](https://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/), [subject 135](https://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/search.php?subjectnumber=135), [boxing category](https://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/search.php?maincat=4&subcat=8).

These recommendations combine local source inspection with upstream documentation research. None of the three external asset pipelines was installed, generated or playtested in this assessment. Approve each pilot on its actual in-game silhouette, animation, collision and performance before expanding production.
